## The thesis in one page (read this first — 5 min)

**What we are trying to achieve:** build the first *outcome-accountable, retention-led, WhatsApp-native* medical clinic for weight, metabolic and longevity care in Southeast Asia — starting in Malaysia, then Singapore, then Hong Kong — using an AI-operated care model that lets a small clinical team deliver continuous, high-touch care at a cost structure incumbents can't match.

**Why now, and why this gap exists:**

- **The demand is epidemiological, not a fad.** In Malaysia, 54.4% of adults are overweight/obese and 15.6% are diabetic; Singapore and Hong Kong are lower but rising fast. GLP-1 drugs (Wegovy, Mounjaro) have just arrived in all three markets. Demand is real, growing, and mostly paid out of pocket — which is the market's normal, not its weakness.
- **The whitespace is an operating model, not a category.** Across ~40 operators in three markets, *no one* combines medical prescribing + retention infrastructure + WhatsApp-native care + published outcomes. Everyone sells a slice — a consult, a pen, a screening, a package — but no one owns the *continuous relationship*. And they can't easily start, because their economics (hospital yield-per-admission, aesthetic commission-per-pen, telehealth per-consult) actively punish longitudinal care. We have no legacy P&L to protect.
- **Retention is the whole business.** The drug is a commodity; the money is in keeping patients on successful treatment. The US market (the mature reference) proved every failure mode of getting this wrong — dependence on loopholes, subscription traps, commoditised drug-retail with no moat. Our model is a point-by-point corrective.
- **The window is 6–24 months** before an incumbent (DoctorOnCall, Doctor Anywhere, a pharmacy chain, or a well-funded DTC player) assembles a copy. The defensible move is to build the slow-to-copy assets first: **published local outcomes** (only one credible study exists across all three markets today), WhatsApp clinical operations, and locked clinical/fulfilment partners.

**The shape of the business (illustrative):** a RM999/month flat-price core weight programme in Malaysia, a longevity membership above it, corporate/insurer channels beside it — scaling to a base case of ~US$28M revenue by Year 3 and ~US$88M by Year 5 across the three markets, on ~US$5M of peak funding, with retention as the single variable that makes or breaks it.

> If you read nothing else, read the two documents in Block 1. Everything else is evidence and design underneath this thesis.

## The trust map, in brief (so tomorrow's reading lands correctly)

- **Solid (corroborated):** the epidemiology, GLP-1 launch timing, the regulatory instruments and dates, payer figures, digital penetration, and the major competitor funding rounds. The internal-consistency audit found the 90 documents quote the same facts consistently.
- **Treat as directional (labelled estimates):** all TAM/SAM/SOM market-sizing and the financial model — sound logic, but built on assumptions, not measured. Don't quote a SOM as fact.
- **Challenge these specifically before external use:**
  1. **The NOVI "708 patients / 12.7% weight loss" outcome stat** — our single most-cited competitor outcome claim — **could not be independently corroborated.** Don't repeat it until we pull the paper.
  2. **The GLP-1 retention baseline** — the number the whole P&L hinges on — may be drifting stale (real-world persistence has risen fast). This is the #1 thing to pressure-test.
  3. **Wegovy launch dates** and **two Hong Kong price anchors** need a manufacturer/provider confirmation.
- **Needs a human professional, not more desk research:** legal sign-off (virtual-clinic licensability, e-prescriptions, GLP-1 advertising) and clinical sign-off (titration protocols, escalation rules). Listed in full in [90-verification/regulatory-verification.md](90-verification/regulatory-verification.md).

## Questions worth bringing to the deeper-dive sessions

Grouped by theme, for the weeks ahead. These are the ones I'd want answered if I were underwriting this.

**On the opportunity**
- Is the "no one owns continuity" whitespace durable, or will a funded player (Doctor Anywhere, a pharmacy chain, ORA) close it inside 12 months? What specifically stops them?
- How real is out-of-pocket willingness to pay RM999/month at the scale we need, versus the small early-adopter segment?

**On the model**
- What is the true, defensible boundary between AI and clinician — and does it survive a medical-board and a lawyer's scrutiny in each market?
- Is WhatsApp a moat or a feature? What's the actual switching cost once a competitor copies the channel?
- Retention is the whole business — what's our concrete, evidence-based plan to beat the unmanaged persistence baseline, and how fast can we *prove* it with a published cohort?

**On the numbers**
- What has to be true for the base case (patients/month, CAC, retention)? Which assumption, if wrong, breaks it?
- What's the real drug-cost/margin position once we negotiate with distributors — the model flags this as unvalidated.

**On execution & risk**
- Can we recruit and retain the clinicians (especially with Hong Kong's emigration-driven scarcity)?
- Which partnerships must we lock first (fulfilment, e-prescription rails, bariatric referral, corporate distribution), and who could lock them against us?
- What's the regulatory tail risk — an enforcement action like Singapore's MaNaDr, or an advertising-rule breach — and how do we design it out?

**On the competition to learn from**
- From the US reference market: what exactly do we copy from Hims (platform, brand, retention) and what do we refuse to copy (compounding dependence, subscription traps)? See [80-us-reference-market/lessons-for-welltech.md](80-us-reference-market/lessons-for-welltech.md).
