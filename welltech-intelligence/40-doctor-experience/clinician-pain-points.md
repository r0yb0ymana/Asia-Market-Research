# Clinician Pain Points: Ranked Friction Inventory and the Welltech Doctor Value Proposition

**Abstract.** This synthesis ranks the frictions that define working life for the Malaysian doctors Welltech must recruit — drawing on the workflow evidence in [doctor-workflows.md](doctor-workflows.md), the prescribing analysis in [prescribing-models.md](prescribing-models.md) and the teleconsultation analysis in [telehealth-consultation-analysis.md](telehealth-consultation-analysis.md) — and maps each friction to a specific removal mechanism in Welltech's AI + WhatsApp operating model. The inventory is led not by burnout in the abstract but by its concrete inputs: income pressure from a 34-year fee freeze and TPA extraction (10–15% fee deductions on ~70% of patient volume), unpaid administration (panel portals, claims, e-invoicing, documentation), unpaid asynchronous care on personal WhatsApp numbers, medico-legal exposure amplified by the 2025 MMC digital-MC ban, and the public-sector contract-doctor crisis that keeps feeding disaffected clinicians into the private market. The closing section converts the inventory into a doctor value proposition — the recruiting pitch — with measurable commitments rather than adjectives.

### 1.1 The evidence, friction by friction

**1. Income pressure.** The consultation fee band a Malaysian GP could lawfully charge was set in 1992 (RM10–35) and codified in 2006; it did not move for 34 years while rents, wages and drug costs compounded — the defining economic fact of the profession. Doctors' bodies demanded RM50–150 (FPMPAM) and RM40–125 (GP town hall); the April 2026 revision delivered a RM80 ceiling but kept the RM10 floor, so realised uplift depends on payer mix. Meanwhile the historic shock absorber — drug margin — is threatened by mandatory-prescription and price-transparency moves (see [prescribing-models.md §2](prescribing-models.md)). A GP's income anxiety is therefore double-ended: the fee side was frozen for a generation, and the margin side is now in regulatory play.[^1][^2][^3]

**2. TPA/GL bureaucracy and extraction.** With ~70% of GP patients arriving through TPA panels, the TPA relationship *is* the revenue relationship — and it is documented as extractive: 10–15% deductions from professional fees, registration and renewal charges, onboarding fees to RM5,000, 30–90 day settlement, unilateral formulary directives (2025's generic-only instruction), and clinical interference (GL conditions on anaesthesia choice). The MMA has demanded TPA regulation for roughly three decades; MOH only began building an MCO/TPA registry in late 2025 and the MMC banned fee-splitting in May 2026 — recognition, not yet relief.[^4][^5][^6]

**3. Unpaid administration and data entry.** Layered mandates each add unpaid clinic hours: per-TPA portals and claim formats, PRIMIS for Madani claims, LHDN e-invoicing (clinics >RM500K turnover from July 2025, all from July 2026), SST changes, price-display compliance — on top of clinical documentation in fragmented CMS products. International primary-care data puts administrative-task dissatisfaction at 50%, double the dissatisfaction with clinical workload itself; there is no reason to believe Malaysian GPs sit below that line given the mandate stack. *(inference on the last point)*[^7][^8][^9]

**4. Unpaid after-hours WhatsApp load.** The Malaysian norm is that patients message the doctor's personal WhatsApp and receive free asynchronous care — services openly advertise free WhatsApp follow-up, no fee schedule line exists for asynchronous advice, and defence bodies warn about the medico-legal exposure of clinical WhatsApp use outside the record. Asynchronous message growth is a documented burnout mechanism internationally. No Malaysian study quantifies the daily load — a measurement gap Welltech should close with its own data.[^10][^11][^12]

**5. Medico-legal anxiety.** The best doctor-side survey evidence in Malaysian telemedicine: 80.6% of practitioners cite medico-legal issues and consent as the top adoption barrier — ahead of money and technology. The 2025 MC-ban episode ([timeline](telehealth-consultation-analysis.md)) validated the anxiety: a service platforms had productised became a disciplinable act overnight, with individual doctors carrying the exposure.[^13][^14][^15]

**6. Follow-up load without follow-up revenue.** Fee-for-service pays for encounters, not outcomes or continuity; titration checks, results explanations and reassurance messaging generate zero revenue in every existing Malaysian construct — clinic, panel or platform. Platform routing to next-available doctors additionally strips the continuity that QUALICOPC links to GP job satisfaction.[^16]

**7. The volume treadmill.** ~40 consultations/day at <15 minutes each is the measured Malaysian primary-care norm; the Madani scheme's 1,000-patients/month clinic target (~50/day) drew public FPMPAM protest as unsafe arithmetic. Volume at this intensity is not a growth lever; it is the ceiling on care quality and the floor of exhaustion.[^17][^18]

**8. Career insecurity.** The contract system placed a generation of doctors on rolling short-term terms: 23,077 contract MOs by 2021 with a 3.4% permanency conversion rate, ~RM3,500/month junior base pay, and on-call rates near RM9/hour — producing the first physician strike in Malaysian history (July 2021) and 6,417 public-sector exits from 2019–2023. These doctors are now in the private/locum/telehealth labour pool with fully formed grievances about employers.[^19][^20][^21]

**9. Platform gig disillusionment.** Consumer teleconsult prices of RM15–30 minus platform economics imply doctor payouts around RM10–18/consult *(inference — no platform publishes rates)*; against a liquid locum market clearing RM40–60/hour, platform work only pays at sustained 3–4 consults/hour, which queue volatility rarely delivers. Add zero patient ownership and full medico-legal duty per encounter, and platform work rationally remains marginal moonlighting rather than a career.[^22][^23]

**10. Burnout.** The compound outcome, quantified below.

### 1.2 The burnout evidence in detail

| Population | Prevalence | Study |
|---|---|---|
| Medical officers, Malaysian tertiary hospital (n=250) | 25.2% burnout | Coping Skills and Burnout among Medical Officers (2020)[^24] |
| Assistant medical officers, nationwide (n=1,839, 2019) | 30.3% burnout | Scientific Reports (2025)[^25] |
| Malaysian healthcare workers, COVID-19 period | Elevated burnout; mixed-methods national sample | Healthcare/MDPI (2021)[^26] |
| Healthcare providers, web-based cross-sectional | Significant burnout prevalence with workload association | Irish J Med Sci (2023)[^28] |
| Public-hospital professionals (coping study) | Substantial psychological burnout; workload primary stressor | PMC (2025)[^27] |
| Medical students (USM) | 67.9% | Cited in MO coping study — the incoming workforce is pre-burned[^24] |

International cross-sectional data adds the mechanism ranking: among primary-care physicians, dissatisfaction runs 50% for administrative tasks vs 26% for clinical workload — administration, not medicine, is the modifiable half of burnout.[^9] Telehealth-specific mechanisms (documentation growth, interoperability gaps, asynchronous message load) are documented and avoidable by design.[^12]

## 2. Pain → mechanism → Welltech removal map

The core design claim: every top-10 friction is either a **workflow artefact** (removable by AI + WhatsApp-native operations) or an **economic artefact** (removable by contract design). None requires regulatory change to fix.

| # | Friction | Welltech removal mechanism | Operating-model component |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Income pressure | Per-consult + per-programme payouts benchmarked publicly against RM40–60/h locum floor; drug margin stays in the clinical system (owned clinic model, [prescribing-models.md §2](prescribing-models.md)) | Compensation architecture |
| 2 | TPA/GL bureaucracy | Direct-to-consumer subscription revenue — no TPA in the loop; where B2B exists, Welltech (not the doctor) absorbs administration; settlement in days not months | Payments & B2B ops layer |
| 3 | Admin & data entry | AI scribe drafts SOAP note from the WhatsApp/voice thread; auto-coded claims/e-invoices; doctor reviews and signs — target: zero doctor keystrokes outside clinical judgment | AI documentation engine ([60-ai-operating-model](../60-ai-operating-model/)) |
| 4 | Unpaid WhatsApp load | All patient messaging flows through Welltech's WhatsApp Business rails: AI triages, answers protocol questions (dose timing, expected side-effects), batches doctor-worthy items into paid review blocks; personal numbers never exposed | WhatsApp care rails |
| 5 | Medico-legal anxiety | Published clinical protocols (GLP-1 initiation in person; no teleconsult MCs, ever; psychotropics excluded); complete immutable encounter records exportable by the doctor; platform-paid indemnity top-up; medical director owns protocol risk | Clinical governance system |
| 6 | Unpaid follow-up | Follow-up is the paid product: doctors receive recurring income per enrolled programme patient (monthly panel fee per titration patient), not per reactive message | Programme economics |
| 7 | Volume treadmill | Panel sizing by protocol, not queue; 10–15-minute protected consult slots pre-prepared by AI intake; async absorbs the trivial | Scheduling + AI intake |
| 8 | Career insecurity | Real employment options (salaried medical officer roles with CPD budget and APC/indemnity paid) alongside flexible fractional contracts — the anti-contract-system offer to Hartal-generation MOs | Talent model |
| 9 | Gig disillusionment | Named-doctor continuity (patients belong to a doctor's panel); transparent published payout table; guaranteed minimum for scheduled blocks (kills queue-volatility risk) | Marketplace design |
| 10 | Burnout | The sum of 1–9, plus measured workload dashboards (message load, panel size, after-hours activity) reviewed monthly as a clinical-safety metric | Ops analytics |

*(analyst design synthesis — the mechanisms are Welltech commitments to be validated in pilot, not observed market facts)*

## 3. The doctor value proposition (recruiting pitch design)

### 3.1 Positioning line

**"Practise medicine. We do the rest."** — differentiated against three named villains every Malaysian GP recognises: the TPA (extraction + paperwork), the incumbent platform (RM15-consult gig work), and the contract system (insecurity).

### 3.2 The offer, quantified

| Pillar | Commitment (pilot targets) | Anti-pattern it answers |
|---|---|---|
| **Pay** | Effective ≥RM80–120/clinical hour across consult + programme-panel income; published rate card; settlement ≤7 days | RM10–18 implied platform payouts; 30–90 day TPA terms[^4][^22] |
| **Time** | Zero unpaid administration: AI-drafted notes, auto-claims, auto-e-invoice; ≤5 min/day non-clinical work, measured | 50% admin dissatisfaction; e-invoice/portal stack[^7][^9] |
| **Boundaries** | No patient ever holds the doctor's personal number; after-hours AI cover with defined escalation; message load capped per panel | Unpaid 24/7 WhatsApp norm[^10][^11] |
| **Safety** | Protocolised care (CPG-2023-aligned GLP-1 pathway), no teleconsult MCs, indemnity top-cover paid, full record export rights | 80.6% medico-legal anxiety; 2025 MC ban whiplash[^13][^14] |
| **Continuity & meaning** | Named panel; outcome dashboards (weight, HbA1c) showing the doctor their impact | Next-available routing; transactional care[^16] |
| **Career** | Salaried + fractional tracks; CPD allowance; APC/indemnity administration handled | Contract-system insecurity; DIY compliance[^19][^20] |

### 3.3 Segment-specific messaging

| Segment | Size signal | Lead message |
|---|---|---|
| Contract/ex-contract MOs (Hartal generation) | 23,077 contract MOs at 2021; 6,417 public exits 2019–23; 1-in-5 declining permanent posts[^19][^20][^21] | Security + modern practice: "A permanent role in medicine that respects you" |
| Locum-portfolio GPs | Liquid market at RM40–60/h on LocumLah/LocumMY[^29] | Beat-the-locum arithmetic: higher effective hourly, no commute, published rates |
| Established private GPs (part-time attach) | 12,000 clinics; fee-thaw era[^1][^3] | Monetise the follow-up work you already do free on WhatsApp |
| Women clinicians/returners | Flexibility demand visible in locum-app adoption *(inference)* | Fractional panels, async-heavy shifts, school-hours scheduling |

### 3.4 Proof obligations (what Welltech must publish to be believed)

1. **The rate card** — first Malaysian platform to publish doctor payouts (incumbent opacity is the opportunity).[^22]
2. **The admin metric** — measured minutes of non-clinical work per doctor per day, reported quarterly.
3. **The protocol book** — public clinical governance (GLP-1 pathway, MC policy, escalation rules) so MMC-conscious doctors can diligence before joining.
4. **The settlement SLA** — payment timing published and audited.
5. **Message-load caps** — panel-size and after-hours policies in the contract, not the FAQ.

### 3.5 Anticipated objections and answers

Doctor recruitment will meet four predictable objections; the pitch should pre-empt them rather than survive them:

| Objection | Root | Answer built into the model |
|---|---|---|
| "Another platform paying peanuts per consult" | RM15-consult incumbents; payout opacity[^22] | Published rate card; guaranteed minimums for scheduled blocks; programme-panel recurring income — the economics are subscription-funded, not consult-arbitrage |
| "I'll get an MMC complaint for something the app did" | 2025 MC-ban whiplash; 80.6% medico-legal barrier[^13][^14] | Public protocol book; medical director owns protocol risk; no MC-from-teleconsult policy stated in the doctor contract; indemnity top-cover platform-paid |
| "AI will make me the rubber stamp for unsafe automation" | MMC AI-ethics guideline climate (see [regulations §8](../10-market-intelligence/malaysia-regulations.md)) | AI acts only within written protocol; every clinical output is doctor-signed; deviation and override rates are audited and published internally |
| "Telehealth killed my clinic's walk-ins — why help you?" | Clinic-owner channel conflict | Owned-clinic partnership tier: GLP-1 initiation visits routed **to** partner GP clinics; Welltech feeds physical visits rather than cannibalising them |

The fourth answer doubles as a market-entry tactic: routing paid initiation consults into established GP clinics converts potential opposition (12,000 incumbent clinics and their associations) into a distribution and referral network. *(analyst recommendation)*

### 3.6 Recruiting-funnel implications

- **Channel**: the doctor market already organises on Facebook locum groups, Lowyat/Reddit threads and WhatsApp networks — recruitment content should target the exact grievances documented there (84-hour weeks, RM9/h on-call, TPA skims) with numbers, not employer branding.[^5][^21][^30]
- **Credibility carriers**: MMA/FPMPAM-adjacent GP voices and Hartal-era advocates are the influencers of this labour market; an advisory panel drawn from them de-risks the pitch.[^2][^4]
- **Sequencing**: recruit the GLP-1/longevity programme panel first (highest per-doctor economics, protocol-driven, async-heavy — the model showcase), then scale the acute teleconsult layer once the employer brand is proven. *(analyst recommendation)*

## 4. Implications for Welltech — KPI set for the clinician experience

| KPI | Target (Year 1) | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Effective doctor RM/clinical hour | ≥RM100 median | Clears locum benchmark with margin[^29] |
| Non-clinical minutes/doctor/day | ≤5 | The admin-zero promise, measured[^9] |
| After-hours messages reaching a doctor | ≤3/day/full panel | Boundary integrity[^12] |
| Doctor 12-month retention | ≥85% | vs gig-platform churn norms *(no MY baseline exists — Welltech's own metric becomes the industry reference)* |
| Doctor NPS | ≥50 | Recruiting flywheel via word-of-mouth in doctor networks |
| Protocol deviation rate | <2% of encounters | Clinical-governance proof for MMC-conscious recruits |

The strategic point: in a market where the doctor experience is defined by extraction (TPAs), opacity (platforms) and insecurity (the public sector), *the clinician experience itself is a defensible moat* — doctors are the scarce input for GLP-1 and longevity programmes, their networks are small and word travels fast, and no Malaysian operator currently competes on how it treats them.
