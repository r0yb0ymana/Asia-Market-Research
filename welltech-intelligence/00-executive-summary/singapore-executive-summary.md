# Singapore Executive Summary — The Welltech Health Investment & Operating Thesis

**Abstract.** This document is the front door to the Singapore research repository: a synthesis of the master market-intelligence document and the Singapore competitor dossiers (WhiteCoat, NOVI Health, ORA Group, Minmed, MyDoc/Fullerton, the longevity-clinic landscape, the Ezra/Function global benchmark, and the Singapore operations of Doctor Anywhere and Speedoc). The controlling conclusion: Singapore is Welltech's ARPU market, not its volume market — a 6.1-million-person city-state at ~USD 99,000 GDP per capita where obesity is lower than Malaysia's (12.7% BMI ≥30) but rising fast, diabetes carries a 1-in-3 lifetime risk, the population screens religiously and then receives no follow-through, and premium cash-pay willingness is exceptional (GLP-1 programmes clear at S$400–800+/month, longevity programmes at S$4,250–15,000+). The regulator has already cleared the low-quality end of the category: the August 2024 MaNaDr shutdown and the November 2024 MOH–HSA Joint Circular 87/2024 (no remote GLP-1 initiation, POM advertising bans actively enforced) make clinical governance the price of admission and — properly executed — a moat. The generic telehealth war is over (Doctor Anywhere and WhiteCoat own the insurer rails; the state owns routine primary care via Healthier SG and HealthHub); the unowned layer is outcome-accountable programme care in the mass-affluent middle. NOVI Health has proven the model clinically (708-patient published cohort, 12.7% weight loss at 12 months) but is sub-scale and app-walled; ORA has the funnel but not the care layer; the longevity market is a barbell with an empty S$2–6k/yr middle. This summary states the thesis, reconciles the sizing (combined year-3 SOM ~S$10–26m ARR), compresses the rulebook, maps the competition and threat clock, specifies the Welltech Singapore model, contrasts it with Malaysia, ranks the risks, and closes with the load-bearing numbers and the verification queue.

## 1. The one-page thesis

**The opportunity.** Singapore pairs a smaller metabolic-disease base with a far richer payer per affected person. Obesity (BMI ≥30) rose from 10.5% (2019–20) to 12.7% (2023–24) — roughly 430,000 obese resident adults, clustering in the 30–39 peak-earning band — and MOH itself headlines the trend as the concern of its flagship survey. Diabetes carries a 1-in-3 lifetime risk (>400,000 living with it; ~1 million projected by 2050). Critically, Singaporeans already screen: 62.6% chronic-disease screening participation, executive packages selling from S$511 to S$12,888 — and then the system does nothing with the report. The screened-but-unmanaged pool is the acquisition funnel, and it skews toward exactly the high-SES segments that pay privately ([singapore-market-intelligence.md §2, §7](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md)).

**How it differs from Malaysia.** Malaysia is a broad cash-pay volume market with loose enforcement and an empty medical middle; Singapore is a narrow, insurance-and-subsidy-anchored premium market with the region's strictest and most-enforced digital-health regime. Out-of-pocket is only ~25% of health spend (vs 76% of Malaysian private financing); 71% of residents hold Integrated Shield Plans; the state has nationalised cheap preventive primary care (Healthier SG, ~1m enrolled in year one) and crowded out discount telehealth (consults banded at S$10–27). The money moves not through OOP catastrophe but through **claims inflation** — 12–15.5% medical trend in 2025, 16.9% projected for 2026 against ~3% healthcare CPI — which makes employers and insurers, not consumers, the burning-platform buyers ([§2.3](#23-payer-structure-the-2026-reform-and-the-employer-channel) below).

**The window.** Three clocks are running. Regulatory: MOH suspended MaNaDr's teleconsult service in August 2024 (>100,000 consultations of ≤1 minute in a sampled month) and moved to revoke its licence; Joint Circular 87/2024 then barred remote GLP-1 initiation and restated the POM advertising wall — clearing the pill-mill end of the category and vacating its demand. Category: Wegovy (HSA-approved 2023) and Mounjaro (weight indication June 2025) are ramping while consumer drug advertising is suppressed, so the first credible *programme brands* will own recall. Payer: the 1 April 2026 rider reform (deductibles no longer fully coverable; co-pay cap doubled to S$6,000) is actively pushing insured consumers toward transparent, package-priced outpatient prevention ([singapore-market-intelligence.md §4.6, §3.3](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md)).

**The whitespace.** One sentence, from the synthesis of the Singapore dossiers: *no Singapore operator combines published outcomes, WhatsApp-native service quality, subscription retention economics and the employer/claims-trend sale at the mass-affluent price tier* — NOVI has the outcomes (the only published cohort in the market) but one clinic, ~34 staff and an app walled garden; ORA has the acquisition machine but a visibly thin post-purchase care layer and no outcomes; WhiteCoat and Doctor Anywhere own the insurer rails but sell episodic consult capacity, not programmes; Minmed owns screening funnels and a S$150 price floor but a ~1★ app and no brand; and the longevity market is a barbell — S$4,250+ concierge or ≤S$1,700 report-and-goodbye screening — with the S$2–6k/yr productised middle empty ([§4](#4-competition) below).

**The entry model.** Singapore is market #2: Malaysia proves the clinical-ops and WhatsApp machinery cheaply; Singapore arrives with evidence and buys the licence, the margin and the regional credibility asset. Beachhead (months 0–15): an **employer metabolic-health programme** — screen → stratify → medically supervised GLP-1 + coaching — sold to 10–20 mid-size employers and brokers against 12–17% medical trend, with HPB grants co-funding 30–90% of programme costs, delivered from one HCSA-licensed clinic node plus the WhatsApp care layer. Then DTC-lite (physician-fronted, condition-framed), the mass-affluent longevity membership (S$2–6k/yr), and the expat/HNW concierge overlay (1.91m non-residents sit entirely outside the subsidy net). Reference pricing: S$450–700/month all-in weight care — below aesthetic-clinic drug markups, premium to DTC discounters, at or under NOVI's S$430 anchor with a deeper service layer ([singapore-market-intelligence.md §11](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md)).

**The moat.** Compliance-as-brand (HCSA OMS licence + approved Clinical Governance Officer + SMC-grade consult protocol + PDPA-hardened messaging + NEHR-ready records — costs absorbed once that post-MaNaDr buyers actively screen for); outcomes publication to the NOVI standard delivered through a channel NOVI doesn't use; and the retention machinery no Singapore GLP-1 seller runs (ORA's own economics are acquisition-led with LTV capped by unmanaged churn — a competitor matching its CAC but doubling 12-month persistence wins on contribution).

**The prize.** Reconciled bottom-up: a Welltech-relevant digital-care pool of USD 0.4–0.7bn (2024–25) growing low-to-mid teens; Segment A (medical weight loss) SAM S$0.7–1.4bn/yr; combined year-3 SOM **~S$10–26m ARR** — fewer patients than Malaysia at similar revenue, at structurally higher gross margin, plus the licence prestige and investor narrative that de-risk Hong Kong and deepen Malaysia ([§2.2](#22-market-sizing-reconciled) below).

**Why now — the window in six dated facts:**

| Date | Fact | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Jun 2023 | HCSA Phase 2: teleconsultation becomes a licensable service; CGO regime applies | The compliance bar that punishes casual entrants and protects the licensed |
| Aug–Oct 2024 | MaNaDr suspension → intended licence revocation; doctors referred to SMC | The pill-mill end of the category is cleared; its demand vacates toward governance-credible providers |
| 22 Nov 2024 | MOH–HSA Joint Circular 87/2024 | No remote GLP-1 initiation; POM ad rules restated for telemedicine — the rulebook Welltech designs to |
| Jun 2025 | Mounjaro weight-management indication approved (KwikPen via DKSH) | Second obesity-indicated GLP-1 live; category inflection while ads stay suppressed |
| 5 Nov 2025 | Health Information Bill introduced | Mandatory NEHR contribution incoming — architect the stack now, cheaper than retrofit |
| 1 Apr 2026 | IP rider reform in force (co-pay cap S$6,000; deductible no longer fully coverable) | Insured consumers pushed toward package-priced preventive outpatient care — Welltech's exact product shape |

## 2. Market

### 2.1 Demand foundations: smaller base, steeper trend, richer payer

Singapore's disease burden is a quarter of Malaysia's in prevalence and a multiple of it in paying propensity per affected person ([singapore-market-intelligence.md §1–2](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md)):

| Indicator | Value | Trend / note |
|---|---|---|
| Population | 6.11m (4.20m residents + **1.91m non-residents**) | The non-resident third has zero subsidy access — cash/IPMI by construction |
| Median resident age / 65+ | 43.2 yrs / 18.8% | ~1 in 4 citizens 65+ by 2030; ageing at Japan's pace at far higher income |
| GDP per capita | ~USD 99,400 | 332,491 resident USD-millionaires; 2,000+ family offices |
| **Obesity (BMI ≥30), residents 18–74** | **12.7% (2023–24)** ≈ 430k adults | ↑ from 10.5% (2019–20) — the NPHS 2024 headline concern; peak band 30–39 |
| Diabetes | >400k living with it; **1-in-3 lifetime risk**; ~1m projected 2050 | War on Diabetes since 2016; ~430k pre-diabetic *(secondary-sourced)* |
| Chronic-disease screening participation | 62.6% (2023) | High and rising — but uptake skews high-SES (59.6% vs 38.8% low-SES) |
| Asian-cutoff treatable pool | Substantially larger than the 12.7% headline | High-risk threshold BMI ≥27.5 used clinically *(verify in full NPHS 2024)* |

Three structural readings. First, **the screened-but-unmanaged pool is the market**: unlike Malaysia (where the undiagnosed fraction is the funnel), Singapore's paying segments are already screening annually and already generating actionable abnormal results — the classic journey is screen → PDF → nothing. The conversion product, not the awareness product, wins. Second, **obesity is framed by the state as chronic disease** (War on Diabetes, Nutri-Grade, Healthier SG), which legitimises clinically serious programmes and punishes cosmetic quick-fix positioning — regulator posture and consumer sophistication point the same way. Third, **the demand segments by payer status, not geography**: one dense city split between subsidised heartland mass (out of scope), IP-insured mass-affluent (core), professional expats (core), and HNW/family-office principals (concierge apex) — perhaps 600k–900k addressable premium adults *(analyst estimate)* supporting price points 3–6× Malaysia's for identical clinical work ([singapore-market-intelligence.md §1.4](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md)).

### 2.2 Market sizing, reconciled

Published headline estimates diverge >10× because scopes differ. Grand View's USD 4.1bn (2023) "telemedicine" figure cannot represent consumer care spend — it exceeds plausible total private outpatient revenue and counts enabling IT and B2B infrastructure. Bottom-up triangulation (≈2–4m teleconsults/yr × S$20–30 + attached pharmacy ≈ S$150–400m) against Statista's USD 671m digital-health envelope yields the working figure ([singapore-market-intelligence.md §5.1](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md)):

| Layer | Estimate | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Headline "telemedicine" (scope-inflated) | USD 4.1bn (2023) → USD 14.3bn (2030) | Grand View; includes IT/infrastructure |
| Consumer digital health | USD 671m (2024) → USD 1.27bn (2029), 13.7% CAGR | Statista |
| **Welltech-relevant digital care** (teleconsults + online pharmacy + digital programmes) | **USD 0.4–0.7bn (2024–25), low-to-mid-teens growth** | analyst reconciliation |

**Segment funnels (TAM → SAM → SOM), all SGD, analyst estimates with stated assumptions** ([singapore-market-intelligence.md §10](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md)):

| Segment | TAM | SAM | SOM (yr 3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Medical weight loss / GLP-1** | ~750k–900k clinically eligible adults; **S$3.4–6.3bn/yr** theoretical | 140k–220k willing/able private payers → **S$0.7–1.4bn/yr** | 1,800–4,000 patients → **S$6.5–14.5m ARR** |
| **B. Longevity & preventive** | 550–700k affluent 35–69 × S$800 avg → **S$0.45–0.55bn/yr** | Mass-affluent S$2–6k tier adopters: 45k–85k → **S$150–350m/yr** | 700–2,500 clients → **S$2–7.5m ARR** |
| **C. Concierge primary care (deliberately narrow)** | ~250–400k concierge-suitable adults | **S$80–200m/yr** | 600–1,500 members → **S$1.5–4m ARR** |
| **Combined** | — | — | **~S$10–26m ARR** |

Sensitivities worth carrying: halving Segment A's willingness rate still yields S$4m+ year-3 ARR from that segment alone; GLP-1 price erosion (semaglutide LOE, oral formulations) cuts drug revenue but *expands* eligible volume — net positive if margin is coaching-weighted, which is the design argument for the programme model; any future insurer/employer coverage of weight-loss GLP-1s (none today — not CDMP-listed) would step-change SAM upward. Segment C functions as the trust wrapper feeding A and B, not a standalone P&L — the generic-consult rails are taken.

### 2.3 Payer structure: the 2026 reform and the employer channel

| Layer | Fact | Welltech read |
|---|---|---|
| OOP share of health spend | **~25%** (down from ~48% in 2000) | Consumers are claimable-anchored and receipt-formal — package pricing and provider legitimacy matter more than in Malaysia |
| Integrated Shield Plans | **71% of residents (~3m)** | "Is it claimable?" shapes private decisions; weight-loss GLP-1s are not — the sale is value, not reimbursement |
| **Rider reform, 1 Apr 2026** | Riders can no longer fully cover the deductible; annual co-pay cap doubles to **S$6,000**; new-style riders ~35–40% cheaper | Re-introduces cost-sharing at private hospitals → insured consumers become receptive to preventive, package-priced outpatient programmes that keep them out of hospital |
| Medical trend | **12–15.5% (2025); 16.9% projected 2026** vs ~3% healthcare CPI; MOH publicly disputes the framing | The employer burning platform; brokers (Mercer Marsh, WTW, Howden) gatekeep wellness procurement |
| MOH budget | S$20.86bn FY2025, **+16.3% YoY** | Fiscal escalation keeps prevention policy-legitimised (Healthier SG, Age Well SG) |
| HPB workplace grants | **30–90% co-funding** of programme costs; corporate wellness ~US$646m by 2030 | Subsidised pilot economics for the employer beachhead |
| Public crowd-out | Healthier SG: ~1m enrolled yr 1, fully subsidised prevention plans; polyclinics/CHAS anchor S$10–30 consults; HealthHub becoming the single national front door | Do not compete on cheap consults or records/booking super-apps — the state owns those layers |

The division of labour is stark: acute episodic GP care, subsidised prevention and chronic CDMP care are state-anchored; **medical weight loss (non-T2DM) and longevity/executive prevention are private-only and unsubsidised** — the two primary whitespaces — with concierge/expat care as the wrapper and the employer channel as the distribution rail ([singapore-market-intelligence.md §3.5](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md)).

### 2.4 Channels and consumer behaviour

- **WhatsApp ~84% of internet users; Telegram ~38%** (unusually strong — channels, communities, privacy) — the SG-specific channel design is WhatsApp as the 1:1 care rail plus a Telegram broadcast/community layer. Internet penetration 98.4%; SingPass/HealthHub familiarity means identity-verified onboarding is tolerated, not a drop-off risk ([singapore-market-intelligence.md §8](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md)).
- Clinics already run bookings, reminders and results informally over WhatsApp (receptionist-on-a-handphone); a local vendor ecosystem exists to professionalise it. Convenience is therefore **not** the differentiator it is in Malaysia — *quality* of the WhatsApp experience is: API-based entity-owned numbers, SLA-ed responses, structured titration journeys, PDPA-clean consent and audit trails ([singapore-market-intelligence.md §9](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md)).
- Behavioural core: kiasu screening culture with no follow-through; insurance-anchored consumption; English as the operating language of healthcare (Mandarin/Malay/Tamil matter for the 60+ cohort, less for the 30–55 core); an expat segment that is concierge-expectant and already WhatsApps its GP.
- Personas that carry the P&L: the post-screening professional (abnormal lipids/HbA1c, S$200–500/mo), the GLP-1-curious executive (S$450–800/mo), the expat family anchor (S$1.5–5k/yr membership), the longevity optimiser (S$3–15k/yr, Chi-price-shocked), the sandwich caregiver ([singapore-market-intelligence.md §8.3](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md)).

## 3. Regulation: the strictest and most-enforced regime in the region

Full analysis: [singapore-market-intelligence.md §4](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md). Singapore, unlike Malaysia, regulates telemedicine through hard law with active enforcement — and the enforcement history is the single most important fact pattern for Welltech's category.

### 3.1 The operating rules

| Domain | The rule as it binds Welltech (July 2026) |
|---|---|
| **HCSA licensing (Phase 2, 26 Jun 2023)** | Teleconsultation is a licensable **Outpatient Medical Service**; platform companies and individual tele-doctors all need licences; services-based, not premises-based. Budget 3–6 months *(analyst estimate)* |
| **Clinical Governance Officer** | Every licensee must appoint a **CGO approved by MOH's Director-General** — recruit a senior SG-registered doctor early; this is the single longest-lead hire |
| **SMC consult standards** | Telemedicine must deliver "the same quality and standard of care as in-person"; doctors and patients must be able to **see and hear each other**; adequate duration; documented consent; MC and prescribing discipline |
| **Joint Circular 87/2024 (22 Nov 2024)** | The post-MaNaDr rulebook: restates telemedicine standards *and* advertising prohibitions together; HCSA licensees **should not initiate GLP-1 injectables via telemedicine** (medications requiring in-use training need an in-person component). This is the proximate driver of ORA's hybrid pivot — and Welltech's design constraint, not obstacle |
| **POM advertising wall** | Advertising prescription-only medicines to the public is **prohibited outright** (HP Advertisement Regulations 2016); MOH/HSA have actively blocked GLP-1 creatives including euphemisms ("weight-loss pen", masked pack shots, "lose 20%" claims) and named platforms (NOVI, Ora Group, Ordinary Folk) in the December 2024 scrutiny. The lawful lanes: factual clinic/programme advertising (HCS(A) Regulations 2021 — no laudatory claims, no inducements), physician-authored disease education, substantiated outcome statistics, HCP-gated detailing |
| **MaNaDr precedent (Aug–Oct 2024)** | >100,000 teleconsults ≤1 minute in a sampled month; one patient with 19 MCs; suspension, intended licence revocation, doctors referred to SMC. MOH's message: telemedicine that functions as a dispensing funnel will be shut down. The anti-MaNaDr build — BMI/comorbidity-gated intake, defensible consult length, titration follow-up, outcome tracking — is simultaneously the compliance posture and the clinical product |
| **PDPA + Health Information Act** | PDPC healthcare guidelines (rev. 2023): WhatsApp Business API on an **entity-owned number**, purpose-specific consent, no ad-hoc identifiable results in chat, retention/deletion policy, audit logs. The HIA (Bill introduced 5 Nov 2025) makes **NEHR contribution mandatory** for all licensed providers including telemedicine — chat-derived clinical facts must reconcile into the record; architect now |
| **Adjacent walls** | APOC pre-clearance for anything aesthetic-adjacent; medication delivery limited to Singapore addresses; overseas providers are unlicensed by definition (cross-border JB corridor = separate licensed entities per jurisdiction with a shared data layer) |

### 3.2 Verdict: compliance as moat

Every load-bearing element of the Welltech design is lawful today, and the constraints select *for* it — more sharply than in Malaysia because enforcement is real and recent. The costs (HCSA licence, approved CGO, video-consult protocol, in-person GLP-1 initiation node, zero-molecule marketing, PDPA-hardened messaging, NEHR-ready EMR) are absorbed once by a governance-led operator and are exactly what cut-corner competitors cannot absorb. The MaNaDr vacuum is an asset: post-2024, employers, insurers and consumers actively discriminate toward governance-credible providers, and the barrier now protects compliant entrants as much as incumbents. The residual exposure is trajectory risk — Circular 87/2024 shows MOH will keep tightening — met by exceeding the current standard by design and publishing the governance model as marketing-grade collateral ([singapore-market-intelligence.md §4.9, §12](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md)).

## 4. Competition

### 4.1 The landscape in one paragraph

The market is **saturated at the rails and empty at the outcomes layer**. Doctor Anywhere (S$88m Series C, 1.5m+ users, S$27.25 consults, seven insurer panels) and WhiteCoat (exclusive AIA partnership; post-Good Doctor acquisition claiming 130+ insurers, 7,500 corporates, 6.8m insured lives) won the insurer-distributed generic telehealth war — but both sell episodic consult capacity with no packaged weight or longevity programme, and both carry review-visible refill and pharmacy-margin friction. The state absorbed routine engagement (Healthier SG, polyclinics, HealthHub on the NEHR spine). NOVI Health is the clinical benchmark: specialist-founded, S$430/month all-in GLP-1 programme, Medisave/CHAS plumbing, a Novo Nordisk partnership (June 2026), and the market's only published outcomes (708 patients, 12.7% at 12 months, IJO 2026) — but one clinic, ~34 staff, a US$5M war chest and an app walled garden. ORA Group (andSons/OVA/Modules; US$17m+, 250k+ consults, >70% subscription revenue) is the strongest DTC acquisition machine and the only player with a hybrid endocrinologist answer to Circular 87/2024 — with a visibly weak post-purchase layer (fulfilment/billing complaints, ~2/5 Trustpilot on a tiny base, no outcomes, no retention machinery). Minmed (29 GP clinics, four screening centres, MOE contract) sets the mass floor: S$21.60 teleconsults and a doctor-prescribed weekly-injection weight programme "from S$150" renewed by WhatsApp video — with a ~1★ app and no brand. Fullerton Health (S$900m+ revenue, founder corruption trial ongoing) and the dormant MyDoc define the corporate channel's strengths and failure modes. The longevity market is a barbell: Chi Longevity (from S$4,250, Four Seasons setting) and hospital executive screening (S$511–12,888, report-and-goodbye) with nothing productised in between ([20-competitor-dossiers/](../20-competitor-dossiers/)).

| Strategic group | Members | Competes on | Structural ceiling |

| DTC subscription telehealth | ORA (andSons/OVA), noah/Zoey, Siena, Trimly/Regimen | Brand, funnel conversion, discreet delivery, price content | Thin longitudinal care; no outcomes; auto-renew friction; LTV capped by unmanaged churn |
| Screening & GP chains | Minmed, ATA, Parkway Shenton, Raffles, Fullerton | Footfall, corporate contracts, price floors | Screening ends at the PDF; no programme layer; experience quality visibly poor (Minmed ~1★ app) |
| Corporate/TPA incumbents | Fullerton, IHP/MHC panels | Panel scale, claims rails, employer inertia | Commodity margins, digital shallowness, governance baggage; can block, cannot build |
| Ultra-premium longevity | Chi Longevity, Regenosis, Artisan, Lifespan Asia | Science halo (Maier/NUS), luxury setting | Capacity-capped, key-person-dependent, S$4,250+ stratum only |

The unowned middle of the value chain — **coaching/retention tied to clinical outcomes** — is the highest-margin, hardest-to-copy layer, and the one MOH's enforcement regime structurally favours because it rewards longitudinal care over transactional consults ([singapore-market-intelligence.md §11.2a](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md)).

### 4.4 Whitespace statement

> **In Singapore, no operator combines published outcomes, WhatsApp-native clinical service, subscription retention economics and the employer/claims-trend sale at the mass-affluent tier. The rails are taken; the outcomes layer is not. The longevity market is a barbell with an empty S$2–6k/yr middle; the weight market has one sub-scale clinical benchmark (NOVI) and a crowd of acquisition machines with no care layer.**

Equally mapped are the dead ends: discount GP teleconsults (state-anchored at S$10–30), MC-adjacent volume plays (the MaNaDr graveyard), drug-name performance marketing (illegal, enforced), records/booking super-apps (HealthHub owns it), hospital-at-home (Speedoc's multi-year moat), and single-ticket S$4,000 scan economics (the Ezra lesson: membership first, scan as attach).

### 4.6 Partnership architecture

The Singapore dossiers converge on the same partner-heavy architecture as Malaysia — own the prescriber relationship, the care channel, the programme layer and the patient data; rent everything else — with SG-specific priorities: **two or more** specialist/GP initiation partnerships (redundancy ORA lacks, required by 87/2024); distributor pharmacy (DKSH) rather than owned dispensing; imaging partners contracted at member rates **before Prenuvo's opening resets prices**; Speedoc for home draws and injection-training visits; broker relationships (Mercer Marsh, WTW, Howden) as the employer gate; and the standing Malaysia rule carried over unchanged — contracts assume the partner becomes a competitor within 24 months, so data, billing and programme IP stay with Welltech and no exclusivity forecloses a second source.

## 5. The Welltech model for Singapore

### 5.1 Entry sequencing

Singapore is deliberately market #2: Malaysia offers volume, looser regulation and cheap iteration to prove the clinical-ops and WhatsApp machinery; Singapore punishes unproven models (licence lead time, enforcement climate, cost base) but rewards arriving with evidence — and its licence + MOH-grade governance is the regional credibility asset read by Hong Kong regulators, insurers and investors alike ([singapore-market-intelligence.md §11.3](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md)).

| Phase | Months | Moves | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Licence & build | 0–6 | Entity; HCSA OMS application; **CGO hired early** (DG approval is the long pole); one clinic node leased (the in-person GLP-1 initiation anchor per 87/2024); WhatsApp/EMR stack localised (PDPA + NEHR-ready); two or more specialist/GP partnerships (redundancy ORA lacks) | Licence granted |
| **Employer metabolic beachhead** | 6–15 | 10–20 employer/broker pilots sold against 12–17% medical trend with HPB 30–90% co-funding; screen → stratify → supervised GLP-1 + coaching; 500–1,500 programme patients; outcome dataset v1 instrumented from day one | ≥60% 6-mo retention; documented %-weight-loss |
| DTC-lite + longevity tier | 15–27 | Physician-fronted, condition-framed content brand (no molecule names, ever); **mass-affluent longevity membership at S$2–6k/yr** reusing the same diagnostics/coaching spine (partner imaging at member rates before Prenuvo lands); concierge/expat membership overlay | CAC payback <9 months |
| Scale & corridor | 27–36 | Insurer wellness-rider conversations (sell outcomes, not consult capacity); JB–SG corridor products with the MY entity; fundraise off SG credibility | S$10m+ ARR run-rate |

**Do not do:** discount teleconsults; MC-adjacent volume; drug-name marketing; aesthetic-style GLP-1 retailing; hospital-at-home; leading with whole-body-MRI claims.

### 5.2 Positioning vs NOVI and ORA

> **For** Singapore's screened-but-unmanaged professionals, insured employees facing rising co-pays, and expats outside the subsidy net, **Welltech is** the doctor-led metabolic-health and longevity programme delivered on WhatsApp **that** turns screening results and GLP-1 prescriptions into measured, published outcomes — named doctors, transparent all-in pricing, proactive weekly care — **unlike** telehealth apps that sell consults, DTC brands that sell subscriptions without care, and screening packages that end at the PDF.

- **vs NOVI — match the evidence, differentiate the channel.** NOVI's own IJO data proves engagement drives outcomes (+2.0–2.2pp for high app-engagers); WhatsApp maximises engagement by deleting the walled-garden onboarding friction. Match or beat the 12.7%/12-month benchmark or reframe the metric; replicate its outcomes-instrumentation discipline and payer plumbing (Medisave/CHAS for chronic lines, employer claims billability, HPB claimability); price at or under the S$430 anchor with a visibly deeper service layer ([novi-health.md §12](../20-competitor-dossiers/novi-health.md)).
- **vs ORA — don't out-brand the brand company; beat it where it publicly loses.** The post-purchase layer is the attack surface: proactive check-ins (days 3/7/14/30), <4h side-effect triage, one-message pause/cancel, published cohort outcomes, honest 6/12-month total-cost publication (ORA hides its ladder behind the funnel). Position on medical accountability — which its "40% of consults end without prescription" statistic gestures at but its care model doesn't deliver ([ora-group.md §19](../20-competitor-dossiers/ora-group.md)).
- **vs the rails (DA/WhiteCoat) — change the procurement conversation.** They sell consult capacity to insurers; Welltech sells outcomes (kg lost, HbA1c, absenteeism, claims offset) to employers — a lane their episodic models cannot service without rebuilding themselves.
- **vs the longevity barbell — compete on operating system, not credentials.** Welltech cannot out-credential Maier/NUS or out-screen IHH; it can out-operate both: membership identity, WhatsApp coordination, subscription billing, GLP-1/metabolic anchoring, partner imaging. Align with and cite the Alexandra/NUS legitimacy layer; never disparage it ([singapore-longevity-clinics.md §14](../20-competitor-dossiers/singapore-longevity-clinics.md)).

Anchored inside the validated corridor — above Minmed's S$150 floor and DTC discounters, below aesthetic-clinic markups and Chi's stratum ([singapore-market-intelligence.md §6.3, §6.6](../10-market-intelligence/singapore-market-intelligence.md)):
