# BookDoc — Competitor Dossier

**Abstract.** BookDoc (operated by Health4U Solutions Sdn Bhd, Malaysia) is the most heavily publicised of Malaysia's first-generation digital health platforms: a doctor-appointment-booking app launched in 2015 by Dato' Chevy Beh (ex-BP Healthcare) that expanded into corporate step-challenge wellness (BookDoc Activ), teleconsultation, and government contracts (PERKESO Activ@Work). Its history is defined by celebrity-investor seed rounds with aggressive valuation claims, a PR-first growth strategy, and a pivot from consumer booking to B2B/government wellness programmes. Founder and CEO Chevy Beh died in August 2022; the company continues to operate — the app was still being updated in July 2025 and the PERKESO Activ@Work Challenge ran again in 2025 — but there is no evidence of new funding, new product direction, or a publicly named successor CEO. For Welltech, BookDoc is a cautionary case study in booking-marketplace economics and a live incumbent in the corporate-wellness channel, not a serious threat in telehealth, weight loss, or longevity medicine.

### Timeline

- **Jul–Oct 2015** — Founded and launched as Malaysia's first online doctor-appointment platform ("ZocDoc for Southeast Asia"). Origin story, heavily used in PR: a friend of Beh's nearly died of misdiagnosed dengue; Beh's personal intervention expedited hospital admission.[^3][^2]
- **Jan 2016** — Seed round led by Brunei's Prince Abdul Qawi (~USD 2M per TechCrunch); BookDoc claimed "the largest seed valuation of any seed-stage startup in Asia" — an unverifiable marketing claim.[^7][^8]
- **2016–2017** — Lifestyle-partner integrations announced in rapid succession: TripAdvisor (medical tourism), Grab, Uber, AirAsia, Agoda; official partner status with SOCSO/PERKESO, FOMEMA and the Ministry of Tourism Malaysia; Berjaya Hotels & Resorts tie-in for tourists.[^9][^10][^11]
- **Feb 2017** — Investment round led by a family member of Macau casino magnate Dr Stanley Ho.[^12]
- **2017–2018** — Launch of BookDoc Activ (step-tracking rewards) and the PERKESO Activ@Work corporate step challenge, which became the company's flagship recurring programme.[^6][^13]
- **Aug 2019** — Publicly "looking to raise Series A from strategic partners" after crossing 100 partnerships in 20+ cities.[^14][^15] No completed Series A was ever announced — a significant signal.
- **Apr 2020** — Strategic collaboration with Tencent-backed WeDoctor: bilingual Global Consultation & Prevention Center offering 24/7 online COVID-19 consultations; covered by CNBC.[^16][^17]
- **Jun 2021** — Forced to deny a "fake news" letter circulating that offered AstraZeneca vaccination at RM100 per jab under BookDoc's name; company stated it was not involved in the national vaccination programme at that time.[^18]
- **Jul 2022** — Beh told Bernama/Borneo Post the platform had passed 1 million users/downloads across seven modules and would invest RM4M in technology in 2023.[^19][^20]
- **Aug 2022** — Founder-CEO Dato' Chevy Beh died at age 37.[^21][^22]
- **2023–2025** — Company continues to run Activ@Work with PERKESO (5th consecutive year in 2023; 2024 and 2025 editions confirmed via the PERKESO microsite and BookDoc's social channels); consumer app still receiving updates (v5.17.62, 11 July 2025).[^13][^6][^5]

## 2. Founders & leadership

- **Dato' Chevy Beh (founder & CEO until 2022).** Son of the BP Healthcare Group founder; previously MD of BP Healthcare (a large Malaysian diagnostics chain). Harvard-educated, Forbes contributor, highly effective self-publicist — most BookDoc coverage is founder-centred. Died August 2022, aged 37; obituaries credited him with a role in COVID-19 vaccine distribution logistics.[^21][^22][^23]
- **Joel Neoh Eu-Jin (co-founder).** Groupon Malaysia founder and later Fave co-founder; his involvement appears to have been early-stage and non-operational.[^2]
- **Post-2022 leadership: undisclosed.** Extensive searching found no announcement of a successor CEO, no leadership page updates covered in press, and no post-2022 executive interviews. The company operates, but leadership is opaque — consistent with family/estate continuity rather than a venture-backed management rebuild. *(inference from absence of evidence across news searches, 2023–2026)*

- **Analytical note:** a company that announced record seed valuations in 2016–2017 and then never closed a priced institutional round in ten years has, in effect, been valuation-orphaned. The prominent-investor strategy generated headlines, not follow-on capital.

## 4. Business model & revenue streams

Per founder interviews with Digital News Asia, revenue is **B2B-centred**:[^26]

1. **Provider subscriptions** — doctors/clinics pay for appointment-management listing and a CRM feature.
2. **Corporate wellness (BookDoc Activ)** — employers pay for step-challenge programmes, dashboards and "big data analytics"; clients cited include Great Eastern Malaysia, Heineken Malaysia, Khazanah Nasional and Kimanis Power.[^26][^13]
3. **Government programmes** — the PERKESO Activ@Work Challenge (free to employers/employees; PERKESO funds it; RM40,000 prize pool in the current edition) is the anchor engagement, running annually since 2017.[^6][^13]
4. **Rewards/affiliate** — Activ tier rewards (Bronze→Platinum) with 90+ reward partners in F&B, retail, pharmacy across 12 countries; likely funded by partner marketing budgets.[^27][^28]
5. **Consumer booking & teleconsult** — free to patients; teleconsult exists in-app ("Tele-Consult" is one of seven modules) but no public pricing is advertised, and BookDoc is rarely cited among Malaysia's telemedicine leaders.[^19][^29]

Financial data points (all third-party estimates or company statements, unaudited): "just shy of break-even," expected 2020, "now cashflow positive" (DNA, founder statements)[^26]; revenue ~RM3.8M/yr around 2020 (aggregator estimate)[^4]; Growjo estimates USD 5.6M revenue, 48 staff.[^4]

## 5. Product & clinical workflow

Seven claimed modules (as of 2022): Search & Book, Activ, Tele-Consult, Marketplace, Events & News, Health Coaching, COVID-19 features.[^19]

**Patient workflow (Search & Book):** search doctor by specialty/location → view profile → book slot → reminders → integrated directions (Google Maps/Waze), ride-hailing (Grab), and for medical tourists, flights/hotels/attractions (AirAsia, Agoda, TripAdvisor).[^9][^10] The travel integrations are affiliate deep-links rather than clinical workflow — breadth-first, depth-light.

**Activ workflow:** sync steps from Apple Health, Fitbit, Garmin, Mi Band → accumulate tier status (Bronze–Platinum) → redeem partner e-vouchers; employers run leaderboard challenges via promo codes (Activ@Work).[^28][^27][^6]

**Provider network claims — treat as marketing.** "More than 40,000 healthcare providers from reputable hospitals in 20 cities" (SCMP country-report advertorial, 2020)[^10]; named anchor partners include the National Heart Institute (IJN) and KPJ Healthcare in Malaysia, NTUC Unity Denticare and Q&M Dental in Singapore, Quality HealthCare and Town Health in Hong Kong.[^10] No independent verification of the 40,000 figure exists; the number likely counts every listed practitioner in every partner group, not active bookable users. The "1 million users" figure (July 2022) is likewise a cumulative-download claim, not MAU.[^19][^20]

## 6. Technology & AI

- Native iOS/Android apps (developer: Health4U Solutions Sdn. Bhd.); Huawei AppGallery distribution as well.[^28][^20]
- Wearable integrations are the most substantive technical asset (Apple Health, Fitbit, Garmin, Mi Band).[^27]
- "Big data analytics for corporates" is claimed in revenue-model descriptions[^26]; no evidence of ML/AI capability, AI triage, or clinical decision support anywhere in product or press.
- **No WhatsApp-based workflow found** — support runs via call centre (1300-88-2362) and in-app channels.[^1]

## 7. Marketing & positioning

- **PR-first strategy.** BookDoc's dominant marketing channel was press releases (PR Newswire cadence 2015–2021), founder profiles (Forbes Asia custom content, SCMP country reports — i.e., paid/advertorial placements), and award claims (CNBC "World's Top 100 Startup", Frost & Sullivan).[^30][^10][^1] This generated exceptional share-of-voice relative to a tiny funding base, but volume collapsed after 2022.
- **SEO.** Homepage now targets "Find 24/7 Online Doctor in Malaysia" — chasing telehealth intent keywords despite booking heritage.[^31]
- **Social.** Facebook (@BookDoc4u) and Instagram remain active, dominated by Activ@Work challenge promotion (2025 challenge posts; Instagram reels in May 2025).[^5][^32] Historical YouTube content covers the PERKESO partnership.[^33]
- **Positioning drift:** consumer super-app (2015–17) → medical-tourism companion (2017–19) → COVID responder (2020–21) → government/corporate step-challenge operator (2022–present). The PERKESO programme is now the brand's centre of gravity.

## 8. Reviews & reputation

- **Google Play:** 3.9★; review themes centre on step-sync failures (wearable data not syncing, step counts wrong) — i.e., complaints about the Activ rewards mechanics, not about healthcare delivery.[^34]
- **App Store:** listed as "BookDoc - Go Activ Get Rewards" (id1037726744) — the store title itself confirms Activ is the lead proposition.[^28]
- **Install base:** 1M+ cumulative downloads claimed (2022, company figure).[^19]
- **Update recency:** Android v5.17.62 released 11 July 2025 — the app is maintained, not abandoned.[^5]
- **Forums:** no meaningful Reddit/Lowyat discussion found — consistent with low organic consumer mindshare relative to its PR footprint. *(absence-of-evidence finding from targeted searches, July 2026)*
- **Reputation risk event:** the 2021 fake RM100-AstraZeneca letter shows brand recognisability was high enough to be exploited by scammers; company response was fast denial.[^18]

## 9. SWOT

| | |
|---|---|
| **Strengths** | Entrenched multi-year government relationship (PERKESO Activ@Work, national reach, free-to-employer); recognised brand among corporates; wearable-integration capability; app still maintained; award/press legacy usable in B2B sales |
| **Weaknesses** | Founder-dependent company that lost its founder; no institutional capital and no public raise since 2018; consumer booking product commoditised; 3.9★ app with sync complaints; opaque leadership; no clinical depth (no in-house doctors, no pharmacy, no chronic-care programmes); no AI capability |
| **Opportunities** | Convert PERKESO/corporate wellness base into screening, mental health, chronic-disease upsells; insurer wellness programmes (AIA-Vitality-style mechanics it already replicates); medical tourism recovery |
| **Threats** | Loss of the PERKESO contract would remove its anchor; corporate wellness challengers (Naluri's clinical coaching model) and telehealth leaders (DoctorOnCall, Doc2US, Qmed) own adjacent budgets[^29]; platform risk if wearable APIs change; brand fade as PR engine has stopped |

## 10. Current status assessment

**Verdict: active but strategically frozen ("maintained incumbency"), not a zombie.** Evidence for activity: July 2025 app release[^5]; 2025 Activ@Work Challenge live on PERKESO's own subdomain and BookDoc social channels[^6][^5]. Evidence of stagnation: no funding since ~2018[^24]; no new product announcements since the founder's 2022 death; no named CEO; press output near-zero since 2022. The most probable trajectory is continued operation as a services vendor to PERKESO and corporates on the Activ platform, with the consumer booking/telehealth ambitions effectively shelved.

## 11. Implications for Welltech

1. **Do not fear BookDoc in Welltech's core domains.** It has no GLP-1/weight-loss capability, no longevity or preventive-medicine clinical programmes, no concierge layer, no WhatsApp workflow, and no AI. Its telehealth module is vestigial.
2. **Study the PERKESO playbook — then leapfrog it.** BookDoc proved that a Malaysian digital-health startup can win and retain a multi-year national government wellness contract with a gamified step product. Welltech's corporate/insurer channel strategy should assume incumbents like BookDoc own "steps and vouchers," and differentiate on clinically meaningful outcomes (weight, HbA1c, metabolic age) that procurement can't get from a pedometer challenge.
3. **PR-led fundraising is a trap.** BookDoc converted royal/celebrity angels and record-valuation headlines into ten years without an institutional round. Welltech should optimise for revenue-quality metrics that survive diligence, not announcement value.
4. **Key-person risk is real and observable here.** A founder-centric brand stalled the moment the founder was gone. Welltech should institutionalise clinical governance and leadership depth early.
5. **Partnership breadth ≠ moat.** BookDoc's dozens of logo partnerships (TripAdvisor, Grab, Agoda, hotels) produced press but no defensible patient relationship. Depth of clinical relationship (medication, follow-up, outcomes) is the moat BookDoc never built — and the one Welltech should.
