# GetDoc — Competitor Dossier

**Abstract.** GetDoc (getdoc.com / getdoc.co) is a clinic-discovery and appointment platform operated by Singapore-registered Jireh Group Pte Ltd, live in Malaysia and Singapore since 2015. It evolved from a booking directory into a low-cost healthcare-benefits membership (GetDocPlus, from ~S$6/year) plus a clinic payments product (GetDocPay), distributed through affinity partners (UOB, SAFRA, Automobile Association, student groups). It raised little (a reported US$1.6M round; no institutional follow-on), runs on a ~10-person team, and shows clear signs of product stagnation: its consumer Android app's last documented release was March 2021, and press coverage effectively stopped years ago. GetDoc matters to Welltech less as a competitor and more as evidence of how thin-margin directory/benefits models plateau in MY/SG — and as a reminder that "clinic network size" claims (400–1,200+ clinics) can persist on websites long after commercial momentum has gone.

### Timeline

- **Feb 2015** — e27 profiles GetDoc as "Malaysia's latest labour of love in med-tech": three co-founders, concept in development since Oct 2014, targeting ~US$300K 2015 revenue and a US$500K seed raise.[^2]
- **Oct 2015** — App launch in Malaysia and Singapore; positioning: search nearby clinics, compare doctors (location, reviews, insurance coverage), book instantly to skip queues.[^3]
- **Jul 2017** — GetDocPay launched: in-app clinic bill payment trialled at ~100 clinics in Klang; platform then claimed 6,000+ doctors across SG/MY, ~1,200 clinics integrated for booking, 40,000+ registered users.[^7]
- **~2018** — A US$1.6M funding round is referenced in secondary profiles; no primary press release traceable; Crunchbase/Tracxn record no substantive round history.[^8][^1]
- **2019–2020** — Pivot to GetDocPlus, a paid membership giving "members-only corporate rates" at panel clinics; launched in Malaysia January 2020; distribution via UOB (free 1-year membership for cardholders), SAFRA, AA, and student schemes.[^9][^10][^11]
- **Apr 2021** — Last significant PR found: "Get doctors on GetDoc" (Enterprise IT News, reposted by Jireh Group) describing search, booking, teleconsult and GetDocPay.[^12]
- **2024** — Jireh Group "last seen on the Peppol e-invoicing network" October 2024; company registration status live.[^1]

## 2. Founders & leadership

Reporting is inconsistent — itself a signal of how thin coverage is:

- **Woon Shung Toon** is listed as Founder & CEO of GetDoc/Jireh Group on The Org, LinkedIn-derived profiles and rocketreach.[^4][^6]
- Vulcan Post's launch story credits **Jerry Hang** (ex-medical-supplies entrepreneur) as the founder, with now-wife **Chris Ching** as CMO.[^3] e27's 2015 piece also centres Hang's story.[^2]
- Most plausible reconciliation: Woon leads the Jireh Group holding entity; Hang/Ching drove the GetDoc consumer brand early on. *(inference — sources conflict)*
- Jireh Group's wider portfolio: **Hippo** (health-tracking device + app), **Rapha Radiology**, **eLog** — a diversified small health-IT shop rather than a focused venture-scale startup.[^13][^5]

## 3. Funding & investors

- Reported **US$1.6M** raised (~2018) per secondary aggregator commentary; no lead investor ever named publicly; Peachscore/Crunchbase profiles show effectively no recorded round history.[^8][^1]
- No government-grant announcements, no VC names, no valuation claims found in any press.
- **Analytical note:** GetDoc is essentially bootstrapped. Its economics must therefore be judged by the membership/payments products' ability to self-fund a 10-person team — which the app-stagnation evidence suggests is only barely happening.

## 4. Business model & revenue streams

1. **Clinic-side subscriptions** — doctors pay a yearly subscription to be listed/bookable; free for patients.[^3]
2. **GetDocPlus membership (B2C/affinity B2B2C)** — ~S$6/year (some categories S$12/year); members get panel rates such as GP consultation S$15.00 + GST, dental scaling & polishing S$80 + GST, plus discounted specialist consults, health screening (including published Raffles Medical / Raffles Health Screeners rate cards), physio, optical and antenatal services.[^9][^14]
3. **Affinity distribution deals** — UOB cardholders (free 1-year GetDocPlus), SAFRA members, AA members, student schemes; presumably paid for by partners or used as acquisition.[^10][^11]
4. **GetDocPay** — clinic e-payments with loyalty points; marketed to clinics as "connecting your clinic to 22 million bank users."[^7][^15]
5. **Teleconsult** — added to the booking flow (book teleconsults online), but never marketed as a standalone paid product.[^12]

Network claims across sources (all company-originated; treat as marketing): 4,000+ doctors in Singapore (site)[^16]; 6,000 doctors SG+MY and 40,000 registered users (2017)[^7]; 400+ clinics SG and 1,200+ clinics MY (UOB partnership page)[^10]; "more than 1,000 clinics" (LinkedIn).[^17] The spread and the stale 2017 anchors suggest the panel numbers have not been re-audited in years.

## 5. Product & clinical workflow

**Patient flow:** search clinics/doctors by location, specialty, insurance coverage → send enquiry or book slot (or teleconsult) → appointment calendar + reminders → optionally pay via GetDocPay → log of medical visits kept in-app.[^16][^12][^18] Users can book on behalf of family members.[^18]

**GetDocPlus flow:** sign up (promo code from partner or paid) → show membership at panel clinic → pay published member rates. This is a discount-card model, not managed care: no triage, no care coordination, no medication delivery, no outcomes tracking.

**Clinical depth: none.** GetDoc employs no doctors, holds no clinical licences of note, and does not deliver care — it brokers discovery, discounts and payments.

## 6. Technology & AI

- Android app `com.jireh.goseedoc` ("GetDoc - Search and Appointment"); iOS app id987814486; a separate "GetDoc Prime" app exists on Google Play.[^19][^20]
- **Update recency: the clearest red flag.** The last documented Android release is v4.14.14, 9 March 2021.[^19] (An unrelated "GetDoc" document-management product by the same group shows v4.14.4 in software directories.[^21])
- Jireh Group's stated direction is IoT personal-health devices plus cloud platforms (Hippo tracker, Jireh Health app portal).[^5][^13]
- No AI claims anywhere; no WhatsApp-first workflow found — contact is via web forms and in-app enquiry.[^22]

## 7. Marketing & positioning

- Positioning: "deliver a better healthcare experience"; mission to "enrich 100 million lives."[^17] In practice, a utility directory + discount membership.
- **SEO:** getdoc.com country landing pages (Singapore/Malaysia) target "find doctors / book appointment" queries[^16]; notably, a development subdomain (`dev.getdoc.co`) is indexed by search engines — sloppy technical housekeeping consistent with a skeleton team.[^23]
- **Content:** health-article content marketing in earlier years (listicles referencing GetDoc appear in tallypress/Doc2US roundups)[^24]; blog and PR output effectively ceased after ~2021.
- **Social:** Instagram (@getdocsg) exists; no evidence of significant follower counts or recent campaigns surfaced in searches.[^25]
- Press footprint concentrated 2015–2017 (e27, Vulcan Post, Digital News Asia)[^2][^3][^26]; nothing meaningful since.

## 8. Reviews, reputation & status assessment

- No meaningful volume of user reviews, Reddit/forum threads, or complaint coverage was found in targeted searches — GetDoc has low consumer mindshare in both markets. *(absence-of-evidence finding, July 2026)*
- Google Play listing remains live but without notable rating/install visibility in search results; the 2021-era last release implies the store listing survives on minimal maintenance.[^19]
- Corporate registration is live and the group transacted on Peppol as recently as Oct 2024 — the business exists, likely sustained by GetDocPlus affinity contracts and Jireh's other lines (radiology IT, devices).[^1]

**Verdict: quasi-dormant / harvest mode.** Not formally dead — websites, membership schemes and partner pages are live — but the consumer platform has had no visible product investment for roughly five years, no funding, no press, and a ~10-person team spread across multiple product lines. Classic quiet-stall pattern of first-generation MY/SG health directories.

## 9. SWOT

| | |
|---|---|
| **Strengths** | Cheap, self-sustaining benefits product (GetDocPlus) with real affinity distribution (UOB, SAFRA, AA); published panel rate cards incl. Raffles network; dual-market (SG+MY) presence; low burn |
| **Weaknesses** | No capital, ~10 staff; consumer app unmaintained since 2021; stale network claims; no clinical services, no pharmacy, no telehealth depth; founder/leadership story muddled; negligible brand awareness |
| **Opportunities** | Sell/licence the panel network and membership book to an insurer or a scaled telehealth player; SME employee-benefits packaging |
| **Threats** | Free national booking infrastructure in SG (health.gov.sg same-day GP booking) erodes the directory's reason to exist[^27]; scaled players (Doctor Anywhere, Speedoc, DoctorOnCall) bundle discovery + telehealth + delivery; app-store delisting risk from stale binaries |

## 10. Implications for Welltech

1. **Directory + discount-card models plateau at sub-scale in MY/SG.** GetDoc executed the "ZocDoc + benefits card" playbook competently and still flatlined at a ~10-person lifestyle business. Welltech should not treat clinic-discovery or panel-rate discounting as a growth engine — only as an acquisition hook attached to actual care delivery.
2. **Affinity-channel distribution is available and underpriced.** GetDoc got UOB, SAFRA and AA to distribute a S$6/year membership. The same channels (banks, membership clubs, universities) are plausible low-CAC routes for Welltech's screening/longevity entry products — with a far stronger offer behind them.
3. **Audit competitors' network claims by date, not size.** GetDoc's 400/1,200/4,000/6,000 figures trace back to 2017 marketing. When Welltech benchmarks "provider network" claims in the region, insist on booking-integrated (transactable) counts and claim dates.
4. **Watch for asset availability, not competition.** GetDoc's clinic panel, membership book, and GetDocPay clinic relationships are more interesting as acquirable assets or partnership shortcuts (instant MY/SG panel coverage) than as a competitive threat.
