# DoctorOnCall — Competitor Dossier

DoctorOnCall (doctoroncall.com.my; operating entity Health Digital Technologies Sdn Bhd) is Malaysia's largest telehealth and online-pharmacy platform. Founded in May 2016 by two former management consultants, it built its moat through first-mover B2C teleconsultation, an integrated e-pharmacy with nationwide delivery, deep Ministry of Health (MOH) partnerships during COVID-19, and a B2B layer spanning ~10 insurers and 8 third-party administrators (TPAs). It claims 1.9 million registered users, a 500+ GP clinic network, 1,500+ bookable specialists and 20 million+ annual visits, and is expanding into Singapore, Indonesia and the Middle East with backing from Samsung Ventures, Khazanah-linked MTDC and India-listed Fischer Medical Ventures. Its weaknesses — episodic transactional care, a web-first pharmacy UX with fulfilment complaints, no structured medical weight-loss/GLP-1 program, and no WhatsApp-native clinical workflow — define the openings for Welltech. See also [../10-market-intelligence/malaysia-telehealth.md](../10-market-intelligence/malaysia-telehealth.md) and [competitor-comparison.md](competitor-comparison.md).

### Timeline

- **2016** — Founded as Malaysia's first online video/audio medical consultation platform; early traction weak in pure B2C ("only 20% of the market" in first six months), pivoting founders toward corporates and insurers, who became the platform's biggest revenue supporters.[^1][^3][^2]
- **2019** — DOCPod pilot in Langkawi: IoT-equipped walk-in kiosk/mobile clinic for rural chronic-disease vitals (BMI, BP, glucose) with video link to public-clinic doctors; referrals into Klinik Kesihatan for serious cases.[^13][^12]
- **Feb 2020** — Partnership with MOH Malaysia to run the national COVID-19 Virtual Health Advisory: free chat/phone/video access to 20 MOH Family Medicine Specialists, 7 days/week — described as the first government-initiated service of its kind in the region.[^7][^8][^6]
- **2020–2021** — COVID-era surge: ~15 million site visitors in 2020;[^4] monthly average users grew from 600,000 (Jan 2020) to 2.5 million (Jan 2021). Signed with MOH to build an online appointment system for the Klinik Kesihatan (KKM) network; ran drive-through/home COVID testing with Qualitas clinics; COVID-19 vaccination booking including at-home vaccination in the Klang Valley.[^5][^13][^1][^35]
- **2021** — US$122k MTDC grant under MOSTI's National Technology & Innovation Sandbox (NTIS) to develop the DOCPod prototype.[^14]
- **2024 (July)** — India-listed Fischer Medical Ventures Ltd (via subsidiary Time Medical International Ventures) formalised investment in Health Digital Technologies, joining existing investors Samsung Ventures and Khazanah-linked MTDC, to take DoctorOnCall global; press cites active expansion in Singapore and Indonesia and a hospital-group contract win in Saudi Arabia.[^15][^16][^17]

## 4. Business model & revenue streams

DoctorOnCall is a multi-sided platform: consumers (consults + pharmacy + screenings), providers (GP clinics, hospitals, labs paying for demand/listing), and payers (insurers/TPAs/employers paying per-member or per-transaction).

| Stream | Mechanics | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| GP teleconsultation | Consult fees from **RM15** (chat/video/audio); a separate help-centre FAQ documents a **RM7.99 teleconsultation fee** line item (fee levels have shifted over time — RM9 referenced in the FAQ URL slug) | [^21][^22] |
| Specialist teleconsult & booking | Specialist consults **from RM80**; appointment booking across "600+ specialties" and top private hospitals (referral/lead-gen economics with hospital partners) | [^22][^23][^10] |
| Online pharmacy | OTC + prescription medicine, supplements; margin on product plus delivery; same-day delivery Klang Valley/Penang, 2–4h express in major cities | [^1][^25] |
| Delivery fees | Standard **RM25**, free above **RM350** (2024 policy; earlier era: RM10 standard, free above RM120); small-order fee below RM10 basket | [^26] |
| Health screening | D2C lab/screening packages observed from **RM49.90 to RM1,998**; phlebotomist home draws; cancer-marker, chronic-disease panels | [^23] |
| Corporate & insurer (B2B) | Claims "10 insurers and 8 TPAs"; partnerships incl. Allianz, Great Eastern (incl. GE Takaful co-branded portal), PruBSN, AirAsia, Zurich campaign page; employer health-screening programs on-site and via partner labs; corporate medical concierge | [^24][^27][^28] |
| Government (B2G) | MOH virtual advisory, KKM appointment system, NTIS-funded DOCPod, COVID testing/vaccination programs | [^7][^13][^14][^35] |
| Vertical clinics (content-led) | Men's health (ED, premature ejaculation) with discreet consult + same-day medicine; sexual health/STD testing; weight management | [^29][^30][^31] |

**Weight loss / GLP-1:** DoctorOnCall has a weight-loss content hub and offers nutritionist/dietitian guidance "online or in person," plus bariatric-surgery referral content — but **no evidence of a structured medical weight-loss or GLP-1 telehealth program with published program pricing** was found on its site or in press as of July 2026.[^31] Its pharmacy sells medicines only against prescriptions; GLP-1-specific commerce pages were not observed. This is a material gap versus regional players (e.g., Roczen operating GLP-1 programs in Malaysia).

**Subscriptions/memberships:** none observed for consumers; corporate contracts are the recurring-revenue layer. *(inference)*

## 5. Clinical workflow

```mermaid
journey
  title DoctorOnCall consult-to-medicine journey (B2C)
  section Entry
    Web/app landing, choose service: 3: Patient
    Select GP (from RM15) or specialist (from RM80): 3: Patient
  section Consult
    Queue for chat/video/audio consult: 3: Patient
    MMC-registered doctor consults, triages: 4: Doctor
    E-prescription issued if warranted: 4: Doctor
  section Fulfilment
    Partner pharmacy (Type A poison licence) reviews Rx: 4: Pharmacist
    Pack + dispatch (2-4h express KV / same-day / standard): 3: Courier
  section Follow-up
    Order tracking; support via WhatsApp/email: 2: Patient
    Repeat purchase requires re-upload Rx or new consult: 2: Patient
```

- **Channels:** chat, video and phone; web-first with companion apps. Doctors are MMC-registered; the platform advertises consults "within minutes."[^10][^22]
- **Doctor network model:** marketplace, not employed — 100+ GPs and 1,500+ private-hospital specialists consult through the platform; specialists remain hospital-based (DoctorOnCall effectively resells their availability). The Ken reported Malaysian telemedicine platforms operating in a regulatory vacuum, with the Telemedicine Act 1997 neither enforced nor prescriptive on modality/standards — DoctorOnCall has publicly called for regulation of the online healthcare space.[^11][^32]
- **Pharmacy licensing:** DoctorOnCall does not itself hold the retail licence for all fulfilment; it partners with independent pharmacies whose pharmacists hold **Type A Poison Licences**; prescriptions valid 90 days per Poisons Regulation 1989; Group B (prescription-only) items require a valid prescription from an MMC-registered doctor; no sale to under-18s per Poison Act 1952 provisions.[^25][^33] The model drew parliamentary scrutiny in 2020 when the Lanang MP asked how DoctorOnCall could sell prescription drugs online — resolved in practice by the consult-then-dispense flow, but a standing regulatory-overhang risk.[^33]
- **Continuity of care:** transactional. No published evidence of assigned named doctors, longitudinal care plans, or proactive follow-up programs; follow-up is patient-initiated. *(inference from site structure and reviews)*

### 5.1 Journey friction points (patient-experience read)

| Stage | Friction observed | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Booking/triage | No structured intake triage before payment; patient self-selects GP vs specialist | Misrouted consults; RM80 specialist fee risk for issues a GP could solve |
| Consult | Queue-based matching, rotating doctors | No chart memory across episodes; repeat history-taking |
| Payment | Consult fee + platform fee + delivery fee accrue separately | Perceived nickel-and-diming vs all-in program pricing[^21][^26] |
| Fulfilment | Partner-pharmacy hand-off is where reviews report failures (delays up to 15 days) | Trust erosion exactly at the clinical moment[^34] |
| Follow-up | Patient-initiated; support via WhatsApp/email with reported response lag | Chronic conditions leak back to physical GPs[^34][^40] |

## 6. Technology

- **Web-first architecture** (doctoroncall.com.my for Malaysia; doctoroncall.com for Singapore/international), with multiple mobile apps: "DoctorOnCall - Online Pharmacy" (Android `com.docmobile`, ~26k downloads per AppBrain, last updated Jan 2024; a newer `com.doctoroncall.pharmacy` listing exists) and iOS app id1549810871 published by Health Digital Technologies Sdn Bhd.[^34][^38][^39] The fragmentation across app listings and the modest download counts indicate the web funnel, not the app, carries the business. *(inference)*
- **AI claims:** Fischer's investment announcement describes an "AI-driven ecosystem that connects patients, healthcare providers, and insurers" — marketing-level claims; no verifiable consumer-facing AI symptom checker or LLM triage product was found on the Malaysian site as of July 2026.[^16]
- **WhatsApp:** used for **customer support and order handling** (published WhatsApp line 010-2151669), not as a clinical consult channel; consults happen in-platform.[^40]
- **IoT/hardware:** DOCPod kiosks (BP, glucose, BMI + video consult) for rural/public-sector deployments.[^13][^14]

## 7. Marketing & positioning

- **Positioning:** "Malaysia's No. 1 / first and largest digital healthcare platform"; trust anchors are the MOH partnership legacy and MMC-registered doctors.[^10][^7]
- **SEO (primary growth engine):** very large indexed footprint across (a) `/medicine/` commerce pages per product/condition, (b) health-centre condition content (e.g., weight-loss programme, erectile dysfunction, STD testing), (c) `/find-doctor/` directory pages per specialty/hospital/service, (d) health-screening package pages, (e) COVID-vaccine booking pages that captured pandemic-era demand. This condition-page + directory strategy makes it the incumbent for high-intent Malay/English health queries.[^22][^23][^29][^31][^35]
- **Promo/discount culture:** heavy — referral program (RM30 cash rewards; RM12-off signup), Touch 'n Go eWallet co-promo (code TNGDOC10, 10% off capped RM30), coupon-site presence with aggressive advertised codes, seasonal voucher campaigns ("duduk rumah dapat voucher"), corporate promo portal.[^41][^42] Signals price-led acquisition and weak willingness-to-pay discipline in B2C.
- **Social:** Instagram @doctoroncallmy ~13K followers; LinkedIn ~6.9K followers; active Facebook page. Small relative to its traffic — organic search, not social, drives the funnel.[^43][^44]
- **Partnership marketing:** insurer/TPA co-branded portals (e.g., Great Eastern Takaful), Zurich campaign, AirAsia, Touch 'n Go — distribution via payers and consumer platforms.[^27][^41]

### 8.1 Regulatory & reputational context

DoctorOnCall's reputation cannot be separated from Malaysia's regulatory vacuum. Three durable facts:

1. **The Telemedicine Act 1997 is unenforced and non-prescriptive** — it neither defines permitted modalities nor registration pathways for online practice; there is no dedicated Online Healthcare Services (OHS) regulation covering telemedicine and e-prescriptions.[^11][^32]
2. **DoctorOnCall's dispensing model has survived direct political challenge.** The November 2020 parliamentary question from the Lanang MP ("how can DoctorOnCall sell prescription drugs online?") did not result in enforcement action; the consult-then-dispense flow through Type A-licensed partner pharmacists remains the operating consensus.[^33][^25]
3. **The company positions itself as pro-regulation**, publicly calling for OHS rules (The Edge cover story) — a classic incumbent strategy: rules written around the incumbent's architecture raise rivals' costs.[^32]

For reputation, the practical consequence is asymmetric: DoctorOnCall carries quasi-official legitimacy from MOH partnerships while bearing no formal telehealth licensing obligations — but any future OHS regime becomes a compliance re-platforming event for it and every competitor simultaneously.

## 10. SWOT

- **Strengths:** brand trust from government work; full-stack consult→pharmacy→screening; payer contracts; SEO moat.
- **Weaknesses:** service-quality variance at scale; no care continuity; no disease-program products (weight, metabolic, longevity); modest funding vs. regional peers.
- **Opportunities:** insurer digitisation budgets; GLP-1 wave (they own the traffic but not the program); GCC/SEA licensing deals via Fischer; public-sector digitisation (KKM).
- **Threats:** telemedicine/e-pharmacy regulation formalising against their partner-licence model; Doctor Anywhere's better-funded regional push; vertical entrants (weight loss, men's health) peeling off their most profitable categories; marketplace disintermediation by hospitals' own apps.

## 11. Differentiation analysis — where DoctorOnCall is vulnerable to Welltech

1. **Program vs. transaction.** DoctorOnCall monetises visits and baskets. A weight-loss/longevity entrant selling outcomes over 3–12 month programs (GLP-1 titration, labs, coaching) attacks demand DoctorOnCall only serves with content and one-off consults.[^31]
2. **WhatsApp-first vs. web-portal.** DoctorOnCall uses WhatsApp only for support; consults live in a web queue. A WhatsApp-native clinical journey (booking, triage, consult, e-Rx delivery updates, proactive follow-ups in one thread) removes the funnel friction that produces their "unresponsive assistant" complaints.[^40][^34]
3. **Continuity and named clinicians.** Their marketplace rotates doctors; there is no longitudinal relationship. Concierge continuity (same doctor + care team, chart memory) is a clean premium wedge that their unit economics (RM15 consults, discount codes) cannot fund.
4. **Fulfilment experience.** Their pharmacy scale is real but reviews show breakage at the last mile. A narrower formulary (weight/metabolic/longevity) with guaranteed cold-chain and next-day SLAs can out-execute them where it matters clinically (GLP-1 pens are cold-chain).
5. **Where not to fight:** commodity OTC e-pharmacy price/breadth, mass GP teleconsults below RM20, insurer panel volume deals, and SEO for generic condition terms — their scale and payer contracts win there.

## 12. Implications for Welltech

- **Do not compete on cheap consults.** DoctorOnCall has anchored Malaysian teleconsult price perception at RM15 (and promo-discounted below). Welltech should price against outcomes (program fees), never per-consult against this anchor.[^21][^22]
- **Move before they productise GLP-1.** They own weight-loss search traffic and pharmacy rails but have not launched a medical weight-loss program. Expect them to bolt one on; Welltech's window is to establish the clinical-program brand (baseline labs, titration protocols, clinician continuity) before a "DoctorOnCall Weight Loss" SKU appears.[^31]
- **Exploit the trust gap at fulfilment.** Publish delivery SLAs, proactive WhatsApp status updates, and refund guarantees explicitly — the exact failure themes in their reviews.[^34]
- **B2B2C is their fortress; pick niches.** Rather than chasing their 10-insurer panel breadth, target employer/insurer programs specifically for obesity/metabolic risk (claims-cost story), where their generic screening offer is shallow.[^24]
- **Regulatory posture as marketing.** Mirror their compliance architecture (MMC doctors, Type A-licensed pharmacy partners, 90-day Rx rules) from day one and communicate it louder; their model survived parliamentary scrutiny, proving the playbook is defensible.[^25][^33]
- **Track:** (a) any GLP-1/weight program launch on doctoroncall.com.my; (b) Fischer-funded product upgrades ("AI-driven ecosystem") reaching the Malaysian consumer UX; (c) new insurer white-labels; (d) Singapore site expansion into consults (currently pharmacy-led).[^16][^36]
