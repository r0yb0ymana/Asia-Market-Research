# Competitor Dossier: MyDoc & Fullerton Health — Singapore's Corporate-Channel Incumbents

**Abstract.** This combined dossier covers the two companies that defined Singapore's employer/insurer-distributed healthcare channel: **MyDoc** (my-doc.com), the 2012-founded B2B telehealth pioneer that white-labelled virtual care for AIA, Aetna, Cigna, Allianz and Guardian pharmacy before fading from the news after 2021 (PitchBook records a November 2021 acquisition by Hong Kong's Heals Healthcare; both founders have moved on), and **Fullerton Health** (fullertonhealth.com), the 2010/2011-founded corporate-healthcare and TPA giant with 500+ medical facilities across nine Asia-Pacific markets, revenue above S$900M (FY2023), and a turbulent decade of ownership drama — a shelved 2016 IPO, an RRJ Capital-led rescue merger (2022), CPIB corruption charges against all three co-founders (February 2024, trial ongoing as of last reporting), a majority-stake sale to Far East Drug (2024) and a Mitsubishi Corporation minority investment (August 2025). Together they map the strengths and failure modes of the corporate channel Welltech must either penetrate or bypass: MyDoc proves B2B2C telehealth distribution works but monetises poorly; Fullerton proves panel/TPA scale is defensible but operationally brittle, digitally shallow, and reputationally exposed. Neither offers medical weight-loss/GLP-1, longevity or WhatsApp-first care — Welltech's core wedges.

## 1. Why these two companies matter

Singapore's private primary-care market is distributed less through consumer choice than through **employer benefits and insurer panels**: the GP visit is prepaid via managed-care programmes, cashless panel cards and TPA administration. MyDoc and Fullerton Health are the two purest expressions of that channel:

- **MyDoc** was the first Singapore telehealth company built B2B-first — no consumer acquisition, only insurer/employer/pharmacy embeds. It shows what corporate-channel *digital* distribution wins (fast covered-lives growth, near-zero CAC) and what it costs (thin revenue, dependence on partners' budgets, invisibility to end users).[^1][^7][^16]
- **Fullerton Health** is the region's largest vertically integrated corporate-health platform — owned clinics plus panel administration for insurers and employers, with ~500–550 facilities, 4M patient visits/year and >S$900M revenue (FY2023).[^24][^25][^40] It controls the physical layer of the same channel: screening, occupational health, panel GP networks, claims administration.
- Both companies were **partners, not competitors**: MyDoc's provider network included Fullerton, MHC, IHP and Acumed.[^8] The channel is an interlocking ecosystem; Welltech will meet these networks (or their successors) in every enterprise deal.

## A4. COVID-era operations

During 2020 MyDoc reported >160% growth in daily active users and was cited in peer-reviewed accounts of Singapore's tele-monitoring and tele-support scale-up; it also stood up a COVID Home Recovery Programme page as one of the telemedicine providers supporting home-recovery patients.[^14] COVID was peak visibility for MyDoc — and the last period with substantial press coverage.

## A7. Review themes

- **Glassdoor (employees):** 3.5/5 from 31 reviews; 62% would recommend. Negatives: weak management, projects assigned without training, "no salary increments and no benefits" — consistent with a cash-tight B2B startup.[^21]

## A8. MyDoc mini-SWOT (historical, as a model to learn from)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Strengths** | First-mover B2B telehealth; blue-chip insurer/pharmacy logos (AIA, Aetna, Cigna, Allianz, Guardian); near-zero consumer CAC; regional footprint on partner rails; Frost & Sullivan recognition[^5][^8][^9] |
| **Weaknesses** | Revenue never followed distribution (S$1.53M FY2022); no owned clinical assets; brand invisible to end users; funding stalled after 2017; dependent on partners' benefit budgets[^7][^16] |
| **Opportunities (then)** | COVID telehealth surge; employer digital-benefits wave; insurer digitisation budgets[^14] |
| **Threats (realised)** | Better-funded omnichannel rivals (Doctor Anywhere, WhiteCoat) took the same insurer panels with consumer brands attached; B2B platform commoditised; exited quietly ~2021[^15] |

### Implications for Welltech — MyDoc

1. **B2B2C distribution alone is a trap.** MyDoc reached millions of covered lives and still booked ~S$1.5M revenue. Welltech should use employer/insurer channels for *acquisition into its own longitudinal programmes* (weight loss, longevity memberships), not as the monetisation layer itself.
2. **The insurer relationships MyDoc built are now orphaned.** AIA, Cigna, Allianz and Guardian all demonstrated willingness to embed a third-party virtual-care layer. Those slots have since been contested by Doctor Anywhere/WhiteCoat, but the appetite is proven — Welltech's differentiated clinical programmes (GLP-1, preventive/longevity) are a fresh reason for those partners to take a meeting.
3. **Own the patient identity.** White-labelling erased MyDoc's brand equity. Welltech's WhatsApp-first model keeps the relationship in Welltech's thread even when a corporate pays.

## B4. Founder charges — precise account (neutral; as reported)

- **8 February 2024:** Singapore's Corrupt Practices Investigation Bureau (CPIB) announced charges against former Fullerton Healthcare Group directors **Dr Michael Tan, Dr Daniel Chan and David Sin**, and against **Chiew Yeow Meng Collin (Collin Chiew)**, former CEO of Aon Singapore and later a director of corporate solutions at AIA (Hong Kong).[^37][^38]
- **Alleged conduct:** between 2015 and 2019, gratification totalling **~S$668,000** was allegedly given to Chiew to advance Fullerton's business interests — ~S$348,000 (2015–2018) while Chiew was CEO of Aon Singapore, and ~S$320,000 over four occasions in 2019 (allegedly from Chan) while Chiew was at AIA. Prosecutors allege the bribes were funded through **inflated/falsified entertainment-expense claims** against the company (falsification-of-accounts charges accompany the corruption charges).[^37][^38]
- **David Sin — resolved 21 August 2025:** pleaded guilty to **six counts of falsification of accounts** (approving expense claims he knew were falsified, totalling >S$213,000, in 2019); fined **S$160,000**; five corruption charges and two further falsification counts were **withdrawn**, with acquittal on those; the court noted he did not personally gain. He was the **first of the four men to be sentenced**.[^38][^39]
- **Tan, Chan and Chiew:** reported as **claiming trial** (not guilty pleas) on corruption and falsification charges, with trial reported as commencing around September 2025.[^39] **No verdict for Tan, Chan or Chiew was found in sources available as of July 2026 — case status beyond the trial's start is unknown from this research.** Charges are allegations; all accused are presumed innocent unless convicted.
- Corporate impact: the alleged conduct predates the current ownership; neither Far East Drug's 2024 majority purchase nor Mitsubishi's 2025 investment was derailed, suggesting acquirers ring-fenced legacy legal risk. *(inference)*[^41][^42]

## B7. Regulatory posture

- **Licensing:** Fullerton's Singapore clinics and screening centres operate under the Healthcare Services Act 2020 (HCSA) outpatient medical service licensing regime (premises-to-services transition completed 2023; outpatient licence conditions govern telemedicine as a mode of service delivery).[^52]
- **Healthier SG:** Fullerton GP clinics participate as enrolment/anchor providers in MOH's Healthier SG preventive-care scheme — a structural moat: capitation-style government primary-care flows favour incumbent clinic estates.[^53]
- **National contractor:** COVID vaccination/swab appointments made Fullerton a trusted MOH surge partner, deepening government relationships.[^50]
- **TPA landscape:** Fullerton competes and co-exists with IHP (est. 1994), MHC Medical Network, Alliance Medinet, MiCare and Adept in Singapore's growing insurance-TPA/managed-care market (one vendor forecast projects strong double-digit CAGR through 2030 — vendor estimate, treat with caution).[^54] The 2026 AdMedika acquisition extends the TPA position regionally.[^45]

## B8. Review themes

- **Glassdoor:** **2.1/5 from 284 reviews** (a second Glassdoor cut shows 2.7/273) — roughly 39% below the healthcare-industry average; only **21% would recommend to a friend**. Themes: "bad management, simply a chaos", weak benefits, no work-life balance, workplace politics and preferential treatment.[^55]
- **Google/Yelp (patients):** screening centres (e.g., Ngee Ann City) draw complaints of understaffing, 2–3-hour waits even with appointments, unanswered phones/emails, occasional rude staff and one reported misdiagnosis; counterbalanced by some efficient-visit reviews. Overall picture: throughput-optimised, experience-poor.[^56]
- **Doctor sentiment:** the 2016 IPO collapse was accompanied by panel-doctor discontent over fee compression — a recurring theme in Singapore managed care (low panel consult fees squeezing GPs).[^28]

### Implications for Welltech — Fullerton

1. **Fullerton owns the pipes Welltech needs.** Panel administration and screening contracts are where employees first meet private healthcare. Welltech should design GLP-1/longevity programmes that plug in *downstream of* Fullerton screenings (abnormal-result → structured intervention), rather than competing for screening volume.
2. **The experience gap is the wedge.** 2–3-hour screening waits and unanswered phones are the antithesis of Welltech's concierge, WhatsApp-first model. Corporates buying wellness differentiation have a visible pain point to defect from.
3. **Post-scandal procurement caution helps challengers.** Insurers and MNC benefits teams facing an incumbent whose founders are on trial for bribing insurance executives will value clean-governance alternatives in RFPs. *(inference)*

### 2.2 Threat assessment for Welltech

- **MyDoc: threat level LOW.** Not an operating competitor in July 2026 on any evidence found. Residual risk: its playbook (embedded insurer telehealth) is now executed by Doctor Anywhere and WhiteCoat, who inherited the channel.
- **Fullerton: threat level MODERATE, asymmetric.** It will not out-innovate Welltech in GLP-1, longevity or AI-enabled concierge care — nothing in its portfolio points that way. But it can **block**: exclusive panel arrangements, bundled screening + managed-care pricing, and employer inertia can keep Welltech out of benefits programmes. With Mitsubishi capital it could also acquire a digital challenger to close its gap. *(assessment)*

### 2.3 Partnership assessment (employer/insurer channel access)

| Route | Mechanics | Realism |
|---|---|---|
| Fullerton panel enrolment | Welltech clinics/doctors join Fullerton (and IHP/MHC/Alliance) panels for cashless corporate patients | High — standard, non-exclusive; accept panel-fee economics as CAC |
| Screening-to-programme referral | Co-designed pathway: Fullerton screening flags (BMI, HbA1c, lipids) → Welltech weight-loss/longevity programme | Medium — commercially logical for Fullerton's client-retention story; requires data-sharing and revenue-split design |
| TPA claims integration | Welltech services claimable via Fullerton/AdMedika TPA rails regionally | Medium — powerful for regional expansion, slower to negotiate |
| Insurer-direct (bypass) | Emulate MyDoc: embed Welltech programmes directly with AIA/Cigna/Allianz/Prudential, who have proven appetite for third-party digital care | High — the orphaned MyDoc precedent is Welltech's pitch deck |

### 2.4 Implications for Welltech (synthesis)

1. **Treat the corporate channel as acquisition, not business model.** MyDoc died of channel-dependence; Fullerton lives on it but earns commodity margins and poor NPS. Welltech should convert channel-sourced patients into direct, subscription, WhatsApp-anchored relationships.
2. **Sell the outcome layer the incumbents lack.** Neither company offers medical weight loss, GLP-1 protocols, longevity medicine or proactive concierge follow-up. Position Welltech as the *clinical-outcomes layer* on top of incumbent screening/panel infrastructure — partner where useful, differentiate where visible.
3. **Exploit the trust window.** Fullerton's founder trial (verdicts pending as of July 2026) and its 2.1/5 employer rating create a procurement moment for a clean, modern alternative in employer wellness RFPs.
4. **Watch two events:** (a) verdicts for Tan/Chan/Chiew — an adverse outcome could shake insurer contracts loose; (b) Mitsubishi-funded digital M&A — the clearest path by which Fullerton becomes a direct digital competitor.
5. **Confirm MyDoc's ACRA status and Heals Healthcare's Singapore ambitions** before treating the MyDoc asset (brand, integrations, or alumni) as fully off the board.
