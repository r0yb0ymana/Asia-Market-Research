# Competitor Dossiers: Singapore's Longevity-Medicine Landscape

**Abstract.** Singapore is the most institutionally legitimised longevity market in Asia: the world's first public-hospital Healthy Longevity Clinic (Alexandra Hospital/NUHS), a National University of Singapore research engine, the global Healthy Longevity Medicine Society headquartered around NUS professor Andrea Maier — and, sitting on top of that legitimacy layer, a fast-forming private market. The category is anchored by Chi Longevity (Prof Maier's clinic at Camden Medical Centre and the Four Seasons Hotel, flagship programmes from SGD 4,250), flanked by NOVI Health's mid-priced NOVI Longevity tiers (~SGD 1,000–1,850), Osler Health's expat-GP longevity assessments, and the deep-pocketed executive-screening incumbents (Parkway Shenton from SGD 511 to SGD 12,888; Raffles from SGD 1,312). Full-body screening MRI is locally available at SGD 4,388–5,014 (ATA Medical and peers), Prenuvo incorporated a Singapore entity in August 2025, and Ezra remains absent from Asia. A TRT segment (SGD 160–400/month), a thin IV/NAD⁺ layer, and a quality-challenged NMN supplement scene complete the picture. Unlike Malaysia, prices here are largely published — but no player yet runs an affordable Function-Health-style subscription (US$365–499/year); the market splits into ultra-premium concierge (SGD 4,000+) and commodity screening (SGD 500–1,700), leaving the productised mid-tier open for Welltech.

### 2.3 Assessment

**Strengths:** the strongest scientific credentialing of any longevity clinic in Asia (arguably globally — the founding president of the discipline's standards body is the founder); luxury distribution via Four Seasons; published pricing (rare in the region); virtual-first programme design that converts medical tourists; diversification into cognitive/physical training via Sparkd. **Weaknesses:** ultra-premium price point caps volume; two boutique sites; heavy key-person concentration in Maier (academic duties at NUS and VU Amsterdam dilute clinic time *(inference)*); no visible pharmacy/GLP-1/metabolic-treatment arm; interventions in longevity medicine remain evidence-emergent, a reputational exposure Maier herself manages by conservative "evidence-based" framing[^4].

## 3. The legitimacy layer: government, academia, standards bodies

This layer does not compete for revenue but sets the evidence bar, trains the clinician pipeline, and normalises consumer demand — a structural advantage no other SEA market has.

| Institution | What it is | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| NUHS Centre for Healthy Longevity (CHL) | Research centre, opened 2022 | Singapore's first Centre for Healthy Longevity; stated goal of adding **five disease-free years** to the Singaporean population's healthy lifespan[^12] |
| Alexandra Hospital Healthy Longevity Clinic | World's first healthy-longevity clinic in a public hospital (launched Aug 2023, with NUHS and NUS Yong Loo Lin School of Medicine) | Accepts patients 35–70 with at most one stable chronic disease; doctors, exercise physiologists, dietitians and health coaches; uses ageing biomarkers calibrated to the Singapore population[^13] |
| — pricing | Baseline assessment + year-long customised programme | ~SGD 1,500–3,000 — a public-sector price anchor roughly one-third of Chi's entry flagship[^14] |
| NUS Medicine longevity trials engine | Academic trial centre attached to NUS Medicine's healthy-longevity programme | Industry research partners include Haleon and Abbott (2025) — evidence the academic layer is monetising via industry trials, not patient fees[^15] |
| Healthy Longevity Medicine Society (HLMS) | Global professional society establishing standards of practice for healthy longevity medicine | Founding President: Andrea B. Maier — the same person anchoring Chi Longevity, giving Singapore standard-setting gravity[^11] |

**Implication for Welltech:** in Singapore the question "is longevity medicine real medicine?" has been answered by the state and the university. A commercial entrant inherits category trust it would have to buy elsewhere — but will also be judged against an unusually literate evidence standard (the AH clinic's inclusion criteria and population-calibrated biomarkers are the benchmark)[^13].

## 4. NOVI Health — the mid-market medical wedge (brief; see separate dossier)

NOVI Health (founded 2018 by three doctors — Dr Sue-Anne Toh, Dr Kyle Tan, Dr Joanne Tay; SGD/USD 5M Series A led by Monk's Hill Ventures) built its business on hybrid diabetes and GLP-1-supported weight-loss care with published real-world outcomes in a multi-ethnic Asian cohort, and has now extended into longevity[^16][^18][^19][^20]. Its **NOVI Longevity** line is tiered — **Start** (gift-certificate price SGD 1,008.26) and **Max** (SGD 1,833.94), with the Max assessment tracking 100+ biomarkers from hormone profiles to VO₂max, plus multi-cancer screening options and physician-led planning; a Visa cardholder promotion bundles a CGM, dietitian consult and two weeks of digital coaching (worth SGD 200)[^16][^17][^21]. NOVI is the closest structural analogue to Welltech in Singapore: clinic + digital coaching + pharmacotherapy + now longevity diagnostics, at roughly one-quarter of Chi's entry price. It is the competitor to model, and the benchmark a Welltech Singapore entry must beat on ops (WhatsApp-first, membership mechanics) rather than on price alone.

## 8. Hormone / TRT clinics

TRT in Singapore is doctor-gated and comparatively transparent. Market guides put all-in costs at **SGD 160–400/month**, with consults SGD 40–120, comprehensive hormone panels SGD 200–500, and topical testosterone from SGD 196.20/month[^37][^38]. Visible players: ATA Medical, PULSE Clinic Singapore (regional lifestyle-clinic network, mirroring its KL operation profiled in the [Malaysia dossier](malaysia-longevity-clinics.md)), Zest Clinic, Dr Tan Medical Center, Cross Street Medical, TAF Clinic, Regis Medical, and **Noah (ofnoah.sg)** — a digital-first men's-health brand whose transparent TRT cost content marketing is the closest local analogue to a telehealth-native funnel[^37][^38][^39]. No player packages TRT inside a broader longevity membership — the cross-sell Welltech's model assumes is unclaimed.

## 9. IV / NAD⁺ lounges

The IV layer is thinner and more medicalised than Kuala Lumpur's. Verified offers: **Drip Hydration** runs mobile IV in Singapore (nurse-to-location; NAD+ Boost US$999, NR US$799, menu US$249–999)[^40]; **Artisan Regenerative Centre** sells clinic-administered NAD⁺ infusions (price unpublished)[^41]; **OXYZ Wellness** markets an IV-drip menu[^42]; REVIV-branded therapy is marketed to Singapore consumers via Harrods' wellness-clinic pages[^40]. No dominant "drip bar" chain surfaced — the segment is an accessory line for clinics, not a standalone category, and Singapore's stricter advertising/clinic regulation plausibly suppresses the KL-style long tail *(inference)*.

## 10. Supplements & biohacking scene

- **Live Long Long** — Singapore longevity-supplement brand founded by Ben Khoo (Singapore Ironman record holder); NMN flagship[^43].
- **Xandro Lab** — budget-positioned longevity supplements, Singapore-based[^45].
- **For Youth** — Singapore NMN/longevity brand producing consumer education (e.g. "Top 10 Biological Age Tests in Singapore")[^45].
- Quality problem: testing of 18 commercially available NMN supplements sold in Singapore found most did not meet label claims — a category-wide trust deficit[^44][^45].
- Media environment: Tatler documents a "biohacking boom" among Singapore's affluent; SCMP covers NMN scepticism; no verified "longevity café" chain surfaced — the café concept remains marketing language rather than an operating category[^44][^45].
- Epigenetics infrastructure: EpiMedTech Global (epiAge) is headquartered in Singapore; TruDiagnostic TruAge kits reach the market via clinics such as MSMC (Dr Chiam Tut Fu)[^50].

## 11. Boutique long tail

Other self-described longevity clinics surfaced: **The Longevity Suite** (Italian biohacking-clinic chain, first Asian flagship at Madison House, Orchard), **The Longevity GP** (aesthetics + anti-ageing GP), **Nuffield Medical's Longevity Clinic (LPA)**, and healthscreening.sg's longevity packages[^46][^47][^48]. None shows Chi-level credentialing or NOVI-level product depth; they matter mainly as noise raising consumer awareness *(analysis)*.

## 14. Whitespace verdict — implications for Welltech

1. **The mid-tier membership is empty.** Between AH's research-gated SGD 1,500–3,000 public programme and Chi's SGD 4,250+ flagships sits the productised, transparently priced annual longevity membership (diagnostics + biological age + physician plan + coaching + re-test). NOVI is closest but sells programmes, not a flagship membership identity. This is the same gap identified in [Malaysia](malaysia-longevity-clinics.md) — but in Singapore it must be executed at a higher evidence standard and price point (plausible anchor: SGD 1,500–2,500/yr *(analyst estimate)* benchmarked against NOVI Max, Raffles Executive and AH).
2. **Compete on operating system, not credentials.** Welltech cannot out-credential Maier or out-screen IHH. It can out-operate both: WhatsApp-first coordination, subscription billing, GLP-1/metabolic anchoring, and imaging partnerships — none of which incumbents run natively.
3. **Partner the imaging layer; don't build it.** Ezra's asset-light partner-radiology model (see [Ezra dossier](ezra.md)) localises directly: contract Singapore private radiology capacity (currently selling full-body MRI at SGD 4,388–5,014) into a member rate before Prenuvo's site opens and resets prices[^34][^35].
4. **TRT and screening-conversion are the immediate wedges.** Monthly-recurring TRT (SGD 160–400/mo unit economics already proven) and a "your Screen Excelsior/Raffles results, turned into a year-long plan" conversion product monetise existing demand without new-category education[^26][^37].
5. **Use Singapore as the credibility flagship, Malaysia as the volume engine.** The HLMS/NUS halo and published-price culture make Singapore the right place to certify the model; the [Malaysian market's](../10-market-intelligence/malaysia-longevity-market.md) opaque boutiques are where the same product wins fastest on transparency. Cross-border virtual programmes (Chi already runs a two-visit, 10-month virtual model) validate a regional telehealth-longevity corridor[^5].
