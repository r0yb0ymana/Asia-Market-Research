# Competitor Dossier: Speedoc

**Abstract.** Speedoc (Speedoc Pte. Ltd., speedoc.com) is a Singapore-founded home-based healthcare and "virtual hospital" provider operating in Singapore and Malaysia (Klang Valley/KL, Penang, Johor Bahru and other cities). Founded in 2017 by Dr Shravan Verma and Serene Cai, it has raised roughly US$33–51M (sources conflict) from Vertex Ventures SEA & India, Bertelsmann Investments, Shinhan Venture Investment and Mars Growth, and has positioned itself as the leading private operator inside Singapore's MOH-endorsed Mobile Inpatient Care@Home (MIC@Home) hospital-at-home programme. Its core economics rest on premium-priced house calls (SGD 327+ per visit), home nursing, non-emergency ambulance, and B2B/institutional contracts with public hospital clusters, IHH's Parkway Shenton, insurers and telcos. It is an acute/episodic and post-acute care specialist — not a longevity, weight-loss or chronic-consumer-subscription player — which defines both its moat (clinical logistics, hospital trust) and its exposure to a WhatsApp-first continuity-care entrant. This dossier covers both its Singapore and Malaysia operations.

**Implication for Welltech:** Speedoc is founder-led, clinically credible, and institutionally embedded, but thinly capitalised relative to Doctor Anywhere and heavily dependent on public-sector programmes for volume.

## 2. Business model & revenue streams

Speedoc is best understood as a **clinical logistics company** with five revenue engines:

| Stream | Description | Buyer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| B2C house calls & home nursing | On-demand doctor visits (8am–midnight MY; 24/7 SG), nurse procedures (wound care, IV, catheter, injections, vaccinations) | Consumers, families of elderly | [^23][^24] |
| B2C telemedicine + medicine delivery | Video GP consults from S$21.80 (SG) / RM30 (MY); free same-day med delivery post-consult (SG); teleconsult fee waived if same-day house call follows | Consumers | [^23][^25] |
| Hospital-at-Home (H-Ward® / MIC@Home) | 24/7 monitored inpatient-substitute care: medical reviews, IV therapy, bloods, remote monitoring; delivered as private clinical partner to NUHS, SGH, KTPH under MIC@Home; subsidies + MediShield/MediSave apply since Apr 2024 | Public hospital clusters / MOH | [^7][^15][^16][^26] |
| B2B2C partnerships | Parkway Shenton (IHH) home care; Great Eastern policyholder teleconsults from S$16; Allianz Malaysia house-visit and women's-health packages; NTUC member deals; StarHub LifeHub+ bundles | Insurers, telcos, primary-care chains | [^13][^17][^27][^28] |
| Ancillary | Non-emergency ambulance (with MOH-registered private partners), CDHM® chronic disease home management, baby vaccination packages, corporate/event medical services | Consumers, corporates | [^9][^10][^29] |

Notable absences: **no online pharmacy marketplace, no mental-health vertical, no aesthetics, and no weight-management or GLP-1 programme** were found on either country site or in press as of July 2026.[^30] Chronic care exists (CDHM®) but is framed as home-based disease management for the already-sick (post-discharge, elderly), not consumer prevention/longevity.

**Implication for Welltech:** Speedoc's model monetises acuity and logistics, not longitudinal consumer relationships. Its B2C funnel is episodic (fever at 2am, post-surgery wound care) with weak repeat-purchase economics outside chronic/home-nursing cases.

## 4. Clinical workflow & doctor network model

**End-to-end flow (B2C):** request via app (iOS/Android) or 24/7 hotline (+65 8180 8948 SG; +60 11 4166 1178 MY) → triage by patient-care team → teleconsult (video, in-app) or dispatch of doctor/nurse → on-site diagnosis, on-the-spot medication dispensing, MCs, lab draws as needed → escalation pathway to ambulance/hospital or step-up into H-Ward monitoring → medicine delivery and follow-up.[^23][^25][^34] Since 2025, an agentic-AI layer conducts symptom checks, gauges urgency, schedules nurse visits or virtual appointments, answers protocol questions from Speedoc's clinical database, and escalates emergencies to clinicians.[^18][^19]

**Workforce model:** house-call doctors are Singapore-licensed, SMC-registered practitioners; the model is a managed network of doctors and employed/contracted nurses rather than a clinic-ownership model — Speedoc owns no consumer clinic estate.[^34] In MIC@Home deployments, the *hospital* care team holds overall medical governance while Speedoc supplements around-the-clock home medical services — i.e., Speedoc acts as clinical subcontractor under hospital protocols.[^26]

**Regulatory posture:**
- **Singapore:** telemedicine and outpatient services are licensable under the Healthcare Services Act 2020 (HCSA), including a "remote" mode of service delivery[^35]; Speedoc additionally operated inside the MOH MIC@Home regulatory & financing sandbox from 2022 until mainstreaming on 1 April 2024, which brought subsidy/MediShield/MediSave parity with inpatient stays.[^15][^16]
- **Malaysia:** no dedicated telemedicine statute is in force (the Telemedicine Act 1997 was never operationalised); practice runs on MMC's Guideline on Telemedicine and the MOH Guideline on Online Healthcare Services (2025), under the Private Healthcare Facilities and Services Act 1998 umbrella.[^36][^37] Speedoc states it is part of MOH Malaysia's regulatory sandbox for telemedicine providers.[^25][^38] **Watch item:** MMC's November 2025 position banning MCs issued after teleconsultations directly hits telehealth conversion economics in Malaysia.[^39]

## 5. Technology & AI; WhatsApp usage

- **Platform:** patient app (iOS/Android) + separate "Speedoc for Licensed Providers" app for the clinician network; remote patient monitoring devices in H-Ward; integrated dispatch/logistics.[^34][^40]
- **Agentic AI (Jun 2025):** upgraded SaaS platform with Searce/Google Cloud under EDB's AI Cloud Take-Off pilot — automated query resolution against clinical knowledge base, AI care coordination (symptom checks, urgency grading, scheduling, escalation), and a continuously learning "bionic memory" fed by regional case data. Positioned explicitly as the scaling lever for home healthcare.[^18][^19]
- **SaaS ambition:** the platform is described as SaaS — signalling intent to sell virtual-hospital infrastructure to other providers/health systems, not just run its own service.[^18]
- **WhatsApp:** no evidence of a WhatsApp-first funnel. Contact model is 24/7 phone hotlines plus in-app booking; customer reviews reference WhatsApp only as an ad-hoc support channel.[^34] *(analyst judgment: WhatsApp is peripheral, not an operating system, at Speedoc)*

## 6. Marketing & positioning

- **Positioning:** "Your Virtual Hospital at Home" / "Healthcare, Brought Home" — clinical, institutional, trust-led. PR gravitates to health-system media (MobiHealthNews, Healthcare IT News, Hospital Management Asia, GovInsider) rather than consumer channels.[^7][^14][^26]
- **Distribution is partnership-led:** public hospital clusters (NUHS, SGH, KTPH), IHH/Parkway Shenton, Great Eastern, Allianz Malaysia (Housevisit; "Wonder Woman"/Allianz Women packages in MY), NTUC deals, StarHub LifeHub+ bundles, Comfort ambulance.[^9][^13][^17][^27][^28][^41]
- **SEO:** country sites (sg.speedoc.com / my.speedoc.com) rank for high-intent queries ("house call doctor Singapore/Malaysia", "doctor home visit JB"); Speedoc appears in third-party listicles for both markets (SmartLocal, MyDocSquad's "Top 10 house-call doctor websites in Malaysia").[^31][^42]
- **Social:** Facebook/Instagram presence (@speedocmed) with founder-story and service content; no evidence of large-scale consumer brand campaigns comparable to Doctor Anywhere's influencer pushes.[^43]

### Customer segments served *(analyst synthesis from service mix and partnerships)*

1. **Families of frail elderly** — house calls, home nursing, CDHM, ambulance; the highest-LTV B2C segment.
2. **Post-discharge / hospital-substitution patients** — H-Ward/MIC@Home, referred and funded institutionally, not self-acquired.
3. **Time-poor professionals and parents** — night house calls, baby vaccinations, teleconsults; episodic, marketing-expensive.
4. **Insurer/telco members** — Great Eastern, Allianz MY, NTUC, StarHub bundles; discounted entry pricing (e.g., S$16 GE teleconsults).[^17][^27][^28][^29]
5. **Expats in Malaysia** — explicitly targeted with English-language teleconsult content on the MY blog.[^25]

Segment 1 and 2 dominate the economics; none of the five constitutes a preventive/longevity cohort, confirming the retention-vacuum thesis in §8.4.

## 8. Competitive context

### 8.1 Market frame

- **Singapore hospital-at-home:** MOH targeted up to 300 MIC@Home virtual beds in 2024 (from 104 in Jan 2024), with full subsidy/MediShield/MediSave parity — a policy-created market in which Speedoc is a named private partner alongside MinMed, Biofourmis and Masimo.[^15][^16]
- **Regional tailwind:** APAC home healthcare projected to exceed US$222.4B by 2033 (11.48% CAGR) — the figure Speedoc itself uses to frame its AI platform investment.[^18]
- **Singapore home-care competitors:** Jaga-Me (acquired by Alliance Healthcare, 2019, ~S$3.5M), WhiteCoat (telemedicine + chronic + mental wellness), MinMed, Doctor Anywhere's house-call service, and hospital clusters' own home teams.[^45]
- **Malaysia house-call competitors:** a fragmented field of local house-call operators (MyDocSquad's directory lists ten), DoctorOnCall (teleconsult ~RM20) and hospital home-care units; Speedoc is the only regional brand with both SG and MY house-call logistics.[^29][^31]

| Force | Assessment |

### 8.4 Customer journey (B2C house call / step-up to H-Ward)

```mermaid
journey
    title Speedoc patient journey (acute home episode)
    section Trigger
      Symptom at home (often night): 2: Patient
    section Access
      App booking or 24/7 hotline: 3: Patient
      Triage by care team / AI assistant: 4: Speedoc
    section Care
      Teleconsult from S$21.80 or house call from S$327: 4: Doctor
      On-the-spot meds, MC, labs: 5: Doctor
    section Escalation
      Ambulance (S$120-170) or H-Ward step-up: 4: Speedoc
      Hospital-governed MIC@Home monitoring: 5: Hospital+Speedoc
    section Aftermath
      Medicine delivery, follow-up teleconsult: 4: Speedoc
      No membership; relationship lapses until next episode: 2: Patient
```

The final step is the strategic point: the journey **ends** rather than converts into a longitudinal relationship — Speedoc has no subscription, panel-doctor continuity, or preventive programme to capture the patient between episodes.

## 9. Strengths / Weaknesses / SWOT

### Strengths
1. Only private operator with deep, named integration into Singapore's mainstreamed hospital-at-home system (MIC@Home) — a regulatory moat competitors cannot quickly replicate.[^15][^16]
2. Real clinical logistics capability: 24/7 doctors, nurses, IV therapy, bloods, ambulance — the hardest layer of home care to build.[^26]
3. Two-market footprint with genuinely localised pricing (RM30 teleconsults, RM250 house calls) and MY MOH sandbox participation.[^25]
4. Credible AI story (agentic care-coordination platform with Google Cloud/EDB backing) aimed at unit-economics, not marketing.[^18]
5. Partnership flywheel with incumbents (IHH/Parkway Shenton, Great Eastern, Allianz MY, StarHub).

### Weaknesses
1. Premium episodic pricing caps B2C volume; no subscription/membership product found to drive retention.
2. No pharmacy marketplace, mental health, weight, aesthetics or longevity verticals — narrow share of consumer health wallet.[^30]
3. Capital position: no new round since Nov 2022; totals (~US$33–51M) far below Doctor Anywhere's (>S$190M raised).[^8][^20]
4. Employer-review signals of leadership/direction problems (Glassdoor 2.9).[^21]
5. Dependence on public-sector programmes concentrates revenue and pricing power with MOH/hospital clusters. *(inference)*
6. Malaysia tele-MC ban (MMC, Nov 2025) weakens a core convenience use-case in MY.[^39]

### SWOT matrix

| | Helpful | Harmful |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | MIC@Home incumbency; clinical home-logistics; agentic-AI ops platform; founder clinical credibility | Episodic economics; no consumer verticals (weight/longevity/mental); thin capital; brand under-investment; employee morale |
| **External** | Ageing SG/MY; MOH push to shift care into homes with full subsidy parity; SaaS export of virtual-hospital stack; JB–SG medical cost arbitrage | Better-funded DA/WhiteCoat expanding into home care; hospital clusters insourcing home services; MY regulatory tightening (tele-MC ban, 2025 online-services guideline); price-sensitive consumers |

## 10. Vulnerability analysis vs a WhatsApp-first continuity-care weight/longevity entrant

1. **Different battlefield.** Speedoc wins acute/post-acute episodes; it has no GLP-1, weight, longevity or preventive subscription product.[^30] A Welltech-style entrant does not need to displace Speedoc to win its category — the direct collision is only in home phlebotomy/nursing logistics, where Speedoc could be a **supplier, not a competitor** (its nurse visits at S$98–131 are a plausible outsourced draw-blood layer for a longevity programme).
2. **Funnel friction.** Speedoc's funnel is app-download or hotline; there is no WhatsApp-native journey. In Malaysia especially, WhatsApp-first onboarding undercuts its acquisition UX.
3. **Retention gap.** No membership plan means no recurring relationship; a continuity-care entrant owns the patient between episodes and can route episodic needs anywhere (including to Speedoc).
4. **Price umbrella.** S$327+/RM250 house calls leave room for lighter-touch continuity models (async review + scheduled home draws) at a fraction of the cost.
5. **Where Speedoc is defensible:** anything requiring 24/7 clinical response, IV-level care, hospital governance integration or ambulance — do not attack this; rent it.
6. **Watch:** the agentic-AI SaaS platform could let Speedoc spin up new verticals (or power a rival's ops) faster than its historical product cadence suggests; and its hospital relationships would make it a credible MOH-trusted partner for any future publicly funded obesity/chronic-prevention programmes. *(inference)*

## 11. Implications for Welltech

1. **Partner, don't fight, on logistics.** Speedoc's nurses/phlebotomy/house-call network in SG, KL and JB is the fastest route to home blood panels and injectable-support visits for a GLP-1/longevity programme — negotiate B2B rates against their published B2C prices (S$98.10 nurse visits; RM250 MY house calls).
2. **Exploit the retention vacuum.** Build the WhatsApp-first longitudinal layer (weight, labs, GLP-1 titration, longevity screening cadence) that Speedoc structurally lacks; position Speedoc-like services as interchangeable fulfilment.
3. **Do not enter hospital-at-home.** MIC@Home incumbency + sandbox history is a multi-year moat; the segment is also subsidy-dependent and margin-opaque.
4. **Price against the umbrella.** Welltech's continuity bundle should cost less per month than one Speedoc house call — a clean marketing contrast ("a year of proactive care for the price of two midnight house calls").
5. **Malaysia regulatory lesson:** MMC's tele-MC ban and the 2025 MOH online-services guideline show MY rules can shift abruptly; Welltech's MY clinical model should anchor on established doctor–patient relationships and physical touchpoints (as MMC guidance prefers) rather than pure cold-start teleconsults.[^36][^39]
6. **Talent watch:** Glassdoor signals suggest experienced Speedoc clinical-ops staff may be recruitable for Welltech's home-services coordination roles. *(inference)*
