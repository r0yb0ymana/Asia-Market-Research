# ORA Group (ora.co / ora.group) — Competitor Dossier: SEA's Vertically Integrated DTC Digital-Health Group, Singapore Focus

**Abstract.** ORA Group is a Singapore-headquartered, vertically integrated direct-to-consumer telehealth company founded in 2020 by ex-Zalora CMO Elias Pour, operating three gender/condition-branded storefronts — **andSons** (men's health), **OVA** (women's health) and **Modules** (dermatology/prescription skincare) — across Singapore, Malaysia and the Philippines. It has raised **US$17M+** (US$10M Series A, May 2023, co-led by TNB Aura and Antler), reports **250,000+ consultations** delivered since its 2021 launch, and earns **>70% of revenue from subscriptions**. In Singapore — its home market and the focus of this dossier — ORA runs a S$20-consult, subscription-medication funnel across ED, hair loss, skin and medical weight loss (GLP-1/GIP), and in **February 2025** launched Singapore's first "hybrid" weight-management service with endocrinologist **Adj. Asst. Prof. Dr Ben Ng** (Arden Endocrinology Specialist Clinic), appointed ORA's first Chief Medical Advisor (now styled Chief Medical Officer on ORA's site). The hybrid move was a direct answer to a hostile regulatory turn: MOH's telemedicine circular effectively barring remote initiation of GLP-1 injectables, a December 2024 Straits Times investigation of weight-loss teleconsult "lapses", and an MOH/HSA statement on prescription-drug advertising in which Ora Group was among the named platforms. ORA is Welltech's closest structural competitor group — same categories, same channels, same regional logic — and this dossier maps where its acquisition machine is strong and its care layer, review base and unit economics are attackable. It builds on, and should be read with, the [OVA (Malaysia) dossier](ova-health.md).

## 5. Business model and revenue streams

1. **Consultation fees** — S$20 one-off video consult per category in Singapore (waived while a treatment in that category is active). Deliberately priced near zero: the consult is a conversion gate, not a profit centre.[^18][^21]
2. **Medication subscriptions** — the core: auto-renewing monthly plans across ED (from **S$32/month**), hair loss, skin, and weight loss (GLP-1/GIP injectables and oral options). Subscriptions were >70% of group revenue in 2023.[^2][^19][^20]
3. **Own-label products and retail** — supplements/haircare under the andSons brand, now distributed through **Watsons Singapore** (brand-listed online), executing the 1,300-store retail plan announced at Series A; Modules sells own-brand skincare (personalised prescription treatment S$49; essentials S$29–39 at 2023 launch).[^2][^16][^22]
4. **Hybrid specialist care (from Feb 2025)** — weight-management packages combining ORA's virtual layer with in-person endocrinology at Arden; pricing not published (see §7).[^8][^9]

The vertical integration claim — consult, prescription, fulfilment and product margin captured in-house — is the group's structural answer to thin telehealth consult margins, and mirrors Hims/Hers rather than Teladoc.[^2]

## 6. Singapore operations (deep dive)

### 6.1 andSons SG (andsons.com.sg) — men's health incl. weight loss

- **Categories:** erectile dysfunction, premature ejaculation, hair loss, skincare, weight loss, well-being/supplements; "100% online", discreet delivery.[^19][^20]
- **Funnel:** online evaluation → mandatory **S$20 video consultation** (auto-added to cart) → doctor approves/declines → subscription delivery.[^20][^21]
- **Weight loss:** injectable (weekly GLP-1/GIP) and oral options; marketing cites clinical-trial averages of **22.5% body-weight loss** for the injectable class (this is the tirzepatide SURMOUNT trial figure, not an andSons cohort — no Singapore cohort outcomes are published).[^19] A "Prescribing of weight-loss medications" position page mirrors the Malaysian brand's screening-rigour messaging.[^23]
- **Entry pricing:** ED treatment plans "from S$32/month"; generic daily tadalafil 5 mg marketed as 32% cheaper than the branded pill.[^20]

### 6.2 OVA SG (getova.com.sg) — women's brand

- **Categories:** weight loss, reproductive health (contraception and related), women's hair loss.[^24][^25]
- **Consult:** **S$20 one-off**, free with an active treatment in the same category; mandatory video consult before any weight-loss prescription; doctor reviews medical history/lifestyle for eligibility.[^26]
- **Weight loss offer:** weekly injectables and daily pills (fat-absorption blocker, i.e., orlistat-class) — "all medications approved by HSA and FDA"; a nutritional guide is emailed and a "dedicated team of dietitians and nutritionists" is claimed, with no published cadence, SLA or named clinicians behind it.[^25][^26]
- **Pricing signals:** OVA's own SG blog frames Mounjaro-under-medical-care at **~S$400–1,200+/month** depending on dose, with lower starting-dose costs — consistent with market benchmarks (§7) but the storefront's exact plan ladder is not search-indexed and the site blocks automated retrieval (see §12 note).[^27]

### 6.3 Modules — dermatology

Launched 2023 in Singapore: personalised prescription skincare (tretinoin, hydrocortisone-level actives) at **S$49** for the custom treatment and **S$29–39** for the three "Skin Essentials" companion products — a deliberate "democratised derm" price point and the group's lowest-friction customer-acquisition brand.[^22]

### 6.4 The February 2025 hybrid weight-management clinic

- **Announced 13 February 2025**: "Singapore's first hybrid weight management care", combining specialist virtual and in-person consultations with "one consistent healthcare team", via partnership with **Arden Endocrinology Specialist Clinic**; **Dr Ben Ng** appointed first Chief Medical Advisor.[^1][^8][^9][^12]
- Content of the model per launch coverage: multidisciplinary evaluation, endocrine work-up, personalised nutrition and exercise planning, and bariatric-surgery counselling/referral where indicated; framed explicitly against Singapore's obesity trend (~12% obese, a further ~30% overweight cited at launch).[^8][^12]
- MobiHealthNews' framing is the honest one: the model exists to **"counter telehealth abuse"** — i.e., to give ORA a compliant pathway for GLP-1 initiation after MOH guidance made pure-remote initiation untenable (§9), and to differentiate from prescription-mill perceptions.[^13]
- In-person care is delivered at Arden's specialist clinic (private endocrinology practice); ORA has **no HCSA-licensed premises of its own surfaced in our review** — the hybrid model rents clinical credibility rather than building clinic infrastructure. *(inference from partnership structure; verify against the HCSA licence register)*[^9][^14][^28]
- **Hybrid package pricing: not published** as of July 2026 — a conspicuous gap given every launch article discusses affordability.

## 9. Regulatory posture (HCSA era) and enforcement exposure

- **Licensing frame:** under the Healthcare Services Act (fully phased in from 2022–23), telemedicine is a licensable mode of service delivery under the Outpatient Medical Service licence, with Clinical Governance Officer requirements; ORA's consults are delivered by SMC-registered doctors under this regime. The specific licensee entity/entities behind andSons/OVA SG were not identifiable from public sources in this review — verify against MOH's HCSA register.[^14][^28] *(unknown)*
- **GLP-1 telemedicine restriction:** MOH Circular No. 87/2024 provides that HCSA licensees **should not prescribe via telemedicine medications such as GLP-1 injections** that require patients to be first trained in their use — effectively mandating an in-person component for initiation. This is the proximate regulatory driver of ORA's February 2025 hybrid launch.[^28][^13]
- **December 2024 scrutiny (advertising):** MOH and HSA jointly reiterated that prescription-only medicine advertising is prohibited in **all** media including telehealth platforms; press coverage of the action named **Novi Health, Ora Group and Ordinary Folk** among platforms whose weight-loss promotions (e.g., "weight-loss pen" euphemisms, masked product imagery, "lose 20% of body weight" claims) drew scrutiny.[^34][^35] This is reputational/warning-level exposure, not a prosecution; no fine, licence action or formal enforcement order against ORA surfaced in our searches. *(as of July 2026)*
- **December 2024 scrutiny (consult quality):** the Straits Times "Teleconsults put to the test" investigation and follow-up reporting ("doctors, patients must see and hear each other during teleconsults; MOH studying potential lapses") put weight-loss teleconsult standards on the public agenda; ORA's 40%-declined statistic and the hybrid model are its two public defences.[^32][^33][^13]
- **Read:** ORA's posture matches its Malaysian pattern documented in the [OVA dossier](ova-health.md) §8A — lawful-aggressive marketing up to the line, followed by fast compliance repositioning when regulators move, and conversion of each compliance upgrade into marketing ("Singapore's first hybrid weight management care"). It is, again, more likely to benefit from a crackdown that clears weaker players than to be a casualty of one.

## 16. Strengths / weaknesses

**Strengths**
1. Only SEA DTC group with a three-brand, three-market platform and >70% subscription revenue — repeatable acquisition machinery.[^2]
2. First-mover compliant answer to MOH's GLP-1 telemedicine restriction (hybrid model + named endocrinologist), convertible into a regional template.[^8][^13][^28]
3. Funding and investor set (TNB Aura, Antler, Gobi) with SEA distribution depth; "largest telehealth Series A in SEA" branding.[^3][^6]
4. Own-label + Watsons retail line diversifies beyond prescription margins.[^16][^22]
5. Publicised screening rigour (40% non-prescription rate) is a genuine trust asset under current scrutiny.[^32]

**Weaknesses**
1. Post-purchase operations are the visibly weak surface: fulfilment delays, billing/auto-renewal complaints, ~2/5 Trustpilot scores on a tiny base.[^38][^39]
2. No published outcomes, retention cadence, or triage SLA in any market; the "dietitian team" claim has no visible structure.[^25][^26]
3. Clinical credibility is rented (Arden partnership), not owned — one partnership dissolution removes the hybrid moat.[^9]
4. Named in MOH/HSA advertising scrutiny (Dec 2024); brand operates close to the compliance line by design.[^34]
5. No WhatsApp-native care, no app, no HK presence; 11–50 staff limits simultaneous execution of GCC ambitions, hybrid rollout and three-market ops.[^15]

## 18. Threat and partnership assessment

- **Threat level for Welltech: HIGH — the highest of any Singapore DTC group.** Same categories (weight/GLP-1, men's/women's health), same subscription mechanics, a 3–4 year brand head start, and the only hybrid-compliance answer already live. If ORA adds retention machinery (proactive care loops, outcomes publication) or lands a Series B, it closes the exact gaps Welltech is designed to exploit.
- **Most dangerous plausible moves (12–24 months):** (1) export of the Dr Ng hybrid template to Malaysia; (2) a longevity/preventive line bolted onto the same funnel; (3) an insurer/employer channel partnership that takes it out of pure DTC.
- **Partnership potential: LOW.** Direct competitor across the core roadmap; no complementary asset Welltech cannot rent elsewhere (specialist partnerships like Arden's are replicable with other endocrinology groups). The realistic non-compete interfaces are talent and, in a consolidation scenario, asset acquisition of a struggling brand line.
- **Contrast:** [Minmed](minmed.md) (infrastructure incumbent) and [MyDoc/Fullerton](mydoc-fullerton.md) (corporate channel) are partnerable; ORA is not.

## 19. Implications for Welltech

1. **Beat ORA where it publicly loses: the post-purchase layer.** Its review record (fulfilment, billing, perfunctory consults) plus zero published outcomes define the service brief: WhatsApp-first proactive check-ins (days 3/7/14/30), <4h side-effect triage, one-message pause/cancel, published cohort outcomes. Nothing in ORA's architecture or headcount suggests it can match this quickly.[^38][^39][^15]
2. **Treat MOH Circular 87/2024 as a design constraint, not an obstacle.** GLP-1 initiation needs an in-person component in Singapore; ORA solved it by renting one endocrinology clinic. Welltech should secure **two or more** specialist/GP partnerships (redundancy ORA lacks) and make the in-person work-up a product feature (baseline labs, body-composition, published protocol).[^28][^8]
3. **Do not out-brand the brand company.** ORA will out-spend and out-polish on gendered consumer brands. Position on *medical accountability* — named doctors, continuity, outcomes — which its 40%-declined statistic gestures at but its care model doesn't deliver.[^32]
4. **Price at the corridor, publish the total.** SG GLP-1 sits at ~S$380–650 entry / S$600–1,200 maintenance; ORA hides its ladder behind the funnel. Publishing honest 6/12-month total cost is a differentiator no cohort member currently offers.[^27][^29][^30]
5. **Move on Hong Kong and the employer channel before ORA does.** Neither is contested by ORA today; both are in Welltech's thesis. Speed there converts ORA from head-on rival to trailing entrant.
6. **Monitoring checklist (quarterly):** Series B or new-market announcements; hybrid-model export to Malaysia; hybrid package pricing publication; any HCSA/MOH enforcement naming ORA entities; Watsons/retail SKU expansion; appearance of retention features (maintenance tiers, outcome dashboards); Dr Ng title/role changes.[^1][^8][^11]
