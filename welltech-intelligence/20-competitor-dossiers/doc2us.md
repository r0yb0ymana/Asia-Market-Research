# DOC2US — Competitor Dossier

DOC2US (doc2us.com; operating entity Heydoc International Sdn Bhd) is Malaysia's leading **text-first** telehealth platform and the country's dominant e-prescription infrastructure player. Founded in 2015 by a practising doctor (Dr Raymond Choy), a startup entrepreneur (Jack Lee) and app developer Agmo Studio, it was the first Malaysian platform to issue digitally signed electronic prescriptions compliant with the Poisons Act 1952 and the Digital Signature Act 1997, and has since wired itself into Watsons, Alpro, BIG and 250+ pharmacy chains, AIA's My AIA super-app, MiCare, and MOH's MySejahtera COVID advisory. Bootstrapped and reportedly cash-flow positive, DOC2US competes less as a consumer brand and more as B2B2C prescription rails — leaving consumer experience, continuity of care, and weight-loss/longevity programs largely unaddressed. Its chat-based DNA makes it the closest structural analogue to a WhatsApp-first model in Malaysia, and therefore the competitor whose moves Welltech should watch most closely on messaging-native care. See also [../10-market-intelligence/malaysia-telehealth.md](../10-market-intelligence/malaysia-telehealth.md) and [competitor-comparison.md](competitor-comparison.md).

### Timeline

- **2015** — Founded; app positioned as "your personal pocket doctor" — press framing: an app that lets you "WhatsApp" doctors anytime.[^1][^9]
- **2017–2019** — Chat consult model matures: GP chat RM9.99 (day)/RM19.99 (overnight); specialists RM19.99/RM39.99; audio and photo messages supported; pay-per-chat via card.[^10]
- **2019–2020** — Launches Malaysia's **first digital-signature-enhanced e-prescription**, working with one of Malaysia's four registered digital-signature providers; compliance framing under Poisons Act 1952 + Digital Signature Act 1997; first Malaysian healthcare app to implement private permissioned **blockchain** for medical-record integrity.[^11][^12][^13]
- **2020–2022 (COVID)** — MOH-linked Virtual Health Advisory delivered **inside MySejahtera** (Digital Health → Virtual Health Advisory → Doc2Us), free and 24/7; COVID-19 Medical Task Force and home-quarantine monitoring; launches **RTKis** (Rapid Test Kit Intelligent System) — AI image-classification of self-test kits with anti-fraud verification, sold at RM45/package; Malaysia Airlines/MAH inbound-traveller testing tie-ups.[^14][^15][^16][^17]
- **Oct 2021** — Regional expansion: technical partner of HOPE Telecare Myanmar (JV with Blue Ocean Investment and TELEMED); ASOCIO 2021 healthtech award; stated pipeline conversations in Cambodia, Laos, Vietnam; Microsoft Azure infrastructure partnership.[^18][^19][^20]
- **May 2022** — **Watsons Malaysia regional partnership**: DOC2US doctors embedded in the Watsons app; free virtual consults with e-Rx dispensed at selected Watsons pharmacy counters.[^21][^22][^23]
- **2022** — Tells The Edge it is seeking USD 8–10M for e-pharmacy R&D, data analytics, and SEA expansion.[^8]
- **Apr 2023** — Launches **DOC2HOME** subsidiary: subscription "Health Concierge" home-based care (doctors, pharmacists, dietitians, nurses, physio, ambulance), first deployed with S P Setia's Setia Eco Park development.[^24][^25]
- **2023–2025** — AIA Malaysia integration into **My AIA** app (virtual consults for policyholders and corporate members, e.g., Mewah Group employee program); MiCare MyMed integration; launches Malaysia's **first fully integrated digital e-referral** solution across clinics, ACCs, hospitals and 2,000+ integrated partner pharmacies; repositions as "Malaysia's Digital Preventive Healthcare Ecosystem."[^26][^27][^5][^29]

## 2. Founders & leadership

- **Dr Raymond Choy Wai Mun** — co-founder & CEO. Medical doctor (10+ years practice, including as a clinic practitioner at Singapore Changi Airport) who saw doctors fielding informal patient/family queries after hours; also motivated by his late father's leukemia and the difficulty of getting second opinions. Left clinical practice in 2020 to run DOC2US full-time. EY Entrepreneur of the Year Malaysia 2023 top nominee; frequent MOH-adjacent commentator (CodeBlue, Jan 2025: telehealth and e-prescriptions "not about replacing traditional care").[^3][^4][^30]
- **Jack Lee (Lee Choon Keat)** — co-founder; startup entrepreneur; commercial/ops side. Tracxn attributes founding to him, illustrating database inconsistency; company materials list Choy, Lee and Agmo Studio jointly.[^31][^3]
- **Agmo Studio Sdn Bhd** — co-founding technical partner (later ACE-Market-listed app developer), explaining DOC2US's early native-app competence.[^3]

A doctor-led leadership identity is DOC2US's key credibility asset with regulators and pharmacy boards — contrast with consultant-led DoctorOnCall ([doctoroncall.md](doctoroncall.md)).

**Pricing observations:** consult price points are deliberately low (≤RM20 GP) and partner-subsidised to free in the Watsons funnel — DOC2US competes on prescription volume and network fees, not consult margin. *(inference from cited pricing)*

## 5. Clinical workflow

```mermaid
journey
  title DOC2US chat-consult and e-Rx journey
  section Entry
    Open DOC2US app / Watsons app / My AIA app: 3: Patient
    Describe issue via text, audio, photos: 4: Patient
  section Consult
    Licensed doctor (or pharmacist/dietitian) responds in chat: 4: Doctor
    Triage; escalate to video or physical care if needed: 3: Doctor
    Digitally signed e-prescription issued in-app: 5: Doctor
  section Fulfilment
    Patient presents e-Rx QR at any authorised pharmacy (Watsons, Alpro, BIG, etc.): 4: Pharmacist
    Or delivery via Alpro + GDEX with tracking: 3: Courier
  section Follow-up
    Chat thread persists in app; refill requests for long-term meds: 3: Patient
```

- **Consult channel:** text chat first (with audio clips and photos), a deliberate asynchronous design distinct from DoctorOnCall's video/call-first queue; video available in integrations such as My AIA.[^1][^26]
- **Practitioner network:** marketplace of licensed doctors, specialists, pharmacists, and dietitians/nutritionists verified against medical-board registration; users can choose preferred providers. Consults asynchronous, so doctors answer around clinic commitments — a moonlighting-friendly supply model.[^1][^36]
- **E-prescription & pharmacy licensing:** DOC2US's core differentiation. Digitally signed e-Rx complies with Poisons Act 1952 and Digital Signature Act 1997 (partnering one of only four licensed digital-signature providers in Malaysia); dispensing happens at licensed partner pharmacies rather than an owned pharmacy, sidestepping the online-dispensing grey zone that drew parliamentary questions at DoctorOnCall.[^11][^12][^6]
- **Continuity of care:** chat history persists and refill delivery exists for chronic meds,[^34] but there is no published named-doctor continuity model, care plans, or outcome tracking; the e-referral product (2025) is the closest move toward longitudinal coordination.[^5]

## 6. Technology

- **Native apps + web app**: iOS (App Store id1009218855) and Android (`com.doc2us.app`), plus web.doc2us.com; built originally by co-founder Agmo Studio.[^36][^37][^3]
- **Digital signature + blockchain**: first Malaysian healthcare app with private permissioned blockchain for record integrity; digital-signature e-Rx stack is the moat asset.[^13][^12]
- **Cloud**: Microsoft Azure partnership for scalability (announced with Myanmar expansion).[^18]
- **AI**: applied, narrow AI — RTKis image-classification engine (ML validation of rapid-test photos with identity/video verification anti-fraud). No public LLM/chatbot triage product found as of July 2026.[^16][^17]
- **WhatsApp**: despite the "WhatsApp your doctor" press framing, consults run in DOC2US's own chat app, not on WhatsApp itself; no evidence of WhatsApp Business API clinical workflows.[^9] *(gap relevant to Welltech)*

## 10. SWOT

- **Strengths:** e-prescription infrastructure monopoly-in-practice; retail-pharmacy embeddedness; clinical founder legitimacy; capital efficiency.
- **Weaknesses:** small team and balance sheet; low consumer brand equity; no program-based products; ageing consult UX economics (≤RM20).
- **Opportunities:** national e-Rx standardisation (they are the de facto standard-setter); pharmacist-led chronic care with Watsons/Alpro; DOC2HOME in ageing-in-place; monetising e-referral rails to hospitals.
- **Threats:** MOH building public e-Rx rails that commoditise their moat; Watsons or AIA insourcing/switching telehealth vendors (concentration risk); better-funded DoctorOnCall/Doctor Anywhere bundling pharmacy + consults; regional players entering with GLP-1/weight programs that pull chronic patients into rival ecosystems.

## 11. Differentiation analysis — where DOC2US is vulnerable to Welltech

1. **Chat-first ≠ WhatsApp-first.** DOC2US validated that Malaysians want asynchronous text care, but forces users into its own app (or a partner app). Welltech running real clinical workflows on WhatsApp itself — zero-download, in the user's default messenger — outflanks the distribution logic DOC2US relies on partners to solve.[^9][^21]
2. **Rails without programs.** DOC2US moves prescriptions; it does not manage conditions. A weight-loss/longevity entrant with protocols, titration, labs and coaching sells outcomes on top of exactly the kind of rails DOC2US built — and DOC2US has no program products to defend with.[^35]
3. **Free-consult economics cap quality.** Free (Watsons) and ≤RM20 consults preclude 20–30 minute clinician time, named-doctor continuity, or proactive follow-up. Welltech's premium continuity model is structurally unreachable for them without breaking partner pricing.[^22][^10]
4. **B2B dependence creates a consumer vacuum.** Because consumers meet DOC2US inside Watsons/AIA properties, there is no strong direct relationship to defend; Welltech can build the direct, high-trust patient relationship DOC2US skipped.
5. **Where not to fight:** e-prescription infrastructure and pharmacy-network integrations. Interoperating with (or emulating the compliance architecture of) digitally signed e-Rx is smarter than rebuilding it; DOC2US's Poisons Act + Digital Signature Act playbook is the reference standard.[^11][^12]

## 12. Implications for Welltech

- **Adopt their compliance architecture, exceed their care model.** Digitally signed e-Rx + licensed-pharmacy dispensing is the regulator-proof pattern in Malaysia. Welltech should replicate it (or partner for it) for GLP-1 and chronic scripts, then differentiate on continuity and outcomes that DOC2US structurally lacks.[^11][^6]
- **Consider them a partner as much as a competitor.** Their 1,500+ pharmacy acceptance network could fulfil Welltech prescriptions in-town where cold-chain courier isn't needed; a rails-level partnership would be consistent with their B2B posture.[^6][^7] *(strategic option — inference)*
- **Move fast on messaging-native care.** DOC2US is one product decision away from a WhatsApp Business API channel; their whole brand was built on the "WhatsApp a doctor" metaphor. Welltech's window on WhatsApp-first is real but not permanent.[^9]
- **Don't copy their pricing.** Free/RM10–20 chat consults are a volume-infrastructure strategy, not a care strategy. Anchor Welltech pricing to programs and continuity, and position DOC2US-style chat as the "commodity tier" of the market.[^10][^22]
- **Watch for:** (a) any DOC2US chronic-disease or weight program (their preventive-ecosystem rebrand hints at ambition); (b) closing of the USD 8–10M round or acquisition by a pharmacy chain/insurer (Watsons and AIA are logical acquirers); (c) MOH national e-Rx policy moves that either enshrine or commoditise their rails; (d) DOC2HOME subscription traction as a concierge-care beachhead.[^8][^24][^30]
