# Competitor Dossier: Ezra (Function Health) — the Global Benchmark for AI-Enabled Full-Body-MRI Longevity Screening

**Abstract.** Ezra (New York, founded 2018 by Emi Gal) built the playbook Welltech's longevity thesis rests on: take an expensive, evidence-contested diagnostic (whole-body MRI), compress its cost with proprietary AI (FDA-cleared "Ezra Flash"), distribute it asset-light through partner radiology networks, and wrap it in a consumer brand. Its price curve — US$1,950 for a 60-minute scan (2019) → US$1,350/30 minutes (2023, post-Flash) → US$499/22 minutes (2025) with a stated 15-minute/US$500 goal — is the single most important datapoint in consumer preventive imaging. In May 2025 Ezra was acquired by Function Health (Jonathan Swerdlin's lab-testing membership platform, backed by a16z and celebrity investors), which in November 2025 raised a US$298M Series B at a US$2.5B valuation and cut its 160+ biomarker membership from US$499 to US$365/year. Together with Prenuvo (US$120M Series B, US$2,499 scans, Singapore entity incorporated August 2025) and Neko Health (Daniel Ek's US$1.7B-valued scan clinics with a 100,000+ waiting list at £299), the model set proves mass-affluent demand for productised, membership-based preventive diagnostics — and simultaneously shows what not to import: single-shot scan economics, evidence backlash, and US-grade CAC. Neither Ezra nor Function nor Neko has entered Asia; Prenuvo is coming to Singapore first.

## 1. Why this dossier exists

Welltech's longevity/preventive product needs an answer to three questions: does anyone pay recurring money for preventive diagnostics at scale; what does AI actually change in the cost structure; and does the model localise to Southeast Asia. Ezra/Function is the cleanest natural experiment on all three, with Prenuvo and Neko Health as the control group.

## 4. Business model

1. **Asset-light imaging.** Ezra owns no scanners. Since 2018 it has partnered with RadNet (346 imaging centres, the largest US outpatient network), Princeton Radiology, Lenox Hill Radiology and others, buying off-peak MRI capacity; outpatient imaging runs up to 40% cheaper than hospital settings[^14][^15]. This is the structural reason it could keep cutting prices.
2. **DTC funnel + membership pricing.** Consumer brand, online booking, 12-month instalment plans, and (post-2025) member pricing through the Function membership[^16][^17].
3. **AI as cost engine, not garnish.** Ezra Flash (an AI image-enhancement model) lets scans run faster at acceptable diagnostic quality: 60→30→22 minutes. Scanner time is the dominant unit cost, so each FDA clearance directly converted to price cuts[^18][^19].
4. **UK internationalisation via the same playbook.** UK launch April 2025 in partnership with Alliance Medical (Marylebone), expanding to 11 sites (Manchester, Newcastle, Glasgow, Taunton, Sidcup…) within roughly eight months, plus an Open MRI option for claustrophobic patients[^20][^21].
5. **Post-merger: diagnostics bundle.** Function sells a US$365/yr membership (originally US$499) covering 160+ lab biomarkers tested twice yearly, with scans (MRI, CT) sold as add-ons at member prices through Ezra's booking rails[^12][^16][^22].

## 6. The Function Health merger (verified)

- **Announced 5–6 May 2025**; financial terms undisclosed. Function (Austin, TX; founded 2022 by Jonathan Swerdlin and co-founders) acquired the NYC-based Ezra outright[^5][^6][^7][^25].
- Immediate launch of the **22-minute, US$499 full-body MRI**, replacing Ezra's US$1,500 base product; available at ~100 US locations at announcement with a stated plan for **1,000+ locations "within months"**[^5][^25].
- Strategic logic: blood biomarkers (Function) + imaging (Ezra) = a single consumer early-detection stack; CNBC framed it as scan-price disruption "for a third of the price"[^6].
- **November 2025:** Series B (US$298M, Redpoint, US$2.5B valuation); membership cut to **US$365/yr**; launch of "Medical Intelligence Lab" (MI Lab) — an AI layer over members' longitudinal health data; Swerdlin: "the most important application of AI — helping people avoid suffering and preventable death"[^12][^13][^22].
- Function claims "fastest-growing health platform in the US" status from its 100+ lab-test membership era[^26].

## 6a. Clinical / product workflow

The end-to-end journey (pre- and post-merger) is the operational template worth studying:

1. **Acquisition** — content-led SEO ("full body scan cost", CT-vs-MRI explainers on ezra.com/blog), press, celebrity/investor amplification; DTC checkout with instalment options[^16][^23].
2. **Screening & eligibility** — online medical questionnaire; physician authorisation of the scan order (US telehealth-prescriber layer standard for elective imaging) *(inference from standard US practice; Ezra markets the scan as physician-reviewed)*[^16].
3. **Scheduling** — member books into a partner imaging centre (RadNet et al.) via Ezra's portal; off-peak slots keep partner economics attractive[^14][^16].
4. **Scan** — non-contrast multi-organ MRI protocol, 22–60 minutes depending on product generation; optional low-dose chest CT/coronary calcium in the Plus tier[^19][^23].
5. **Read & report** — board-certified radiologists read AI-enhanced images; consumer-legible report ("Ezra Report") delivered digitally; findings triaged by severity[^4][^17].
6. **Debrief & follow-up** — clinical team walk-through of results; ~5% of members receive a "highly suspicious" flag requiring diagnostic follow-up outside Ezra — the hand-off point where the model's value chain ends[^17].
7. **Retention (post-merger)** — findings feed the Function dashboard alongside 160+ biomarkers re-tested twice a year; MI Lab layers AI interpretation over the longitudinal record; annual re-scan prompted as membership behaviour[^12][^22].

The critical structural gap: steps 1–6 monetise detection, but treatment, reassurance care and false-positive workups are externalised to the wider health system — the inverse of Welltech's clinic-plus-telehealth model, which can own step 7's economics *(analysis)*.

## 8. Marketing, positioning, review themes

- **Positioning:** "early detection as consumer empowerment", riding the Bryan Johnson/longevity zeitgeist; Fox News and profile-press coverage frame Ezra as democratising elite screening[^4][^29].
- **Celebrity/creator distribution:** primarily via Function's investor-celebrity bench (Damon, Hart, Efron, Pascal) — equity-for-influence as a CAC strategy[^10].
- **Review themes (findable):** independent reviews (Keep Health) note ~**5% of Ezra clients get a "highly suspicious" finding requiring follow-up**, that no major medical society recommends whole-body MRI for asymptomatic screening, and that the service skews to those able to spend US$1,200+/yr out of pocket; first-person accounts (Medium) describe smooth logistics but anxiety around incidental findings[^17][^30].
- **Evidence backlash is the main brand risk:** the ACR's standing statement finds no documented evidence that total-body screening is cost-effective or life-prolonging and warns of cascades from non-specific findings; systematic reviews find substantial false-positive burdens; Fred Hutch and RSNA coverage (2025–26) keep the criticism current[^31][^32][^33].

## 10. Strengths, weaknesses, SWOT (Ezra/Function)

**Strengths:** category-defining price curve; FDA-cleared proprietary AI; asset-light scale (100→1,000 sites); membership economics smoothing scan revenue; a16z/Redpoint balance sheet; celebrity distribution. **Weaknesses:** clinical-evidence deficit for asymptomatic whole-body screening (ACR position) is unresolved and invites regulatory/press risk; ~5% suspicious-finding rate creates downstream-care liabilities the company doesn't own; merger integration risk; US-centric ops; the US$499 scan's margin depends on partner-scanner utilisation staying cheap *(analysis)*[^17][^31].

| | Helpful | Harmful |
|---|---|---|
| Internal | **S:** AI cost engine; asset-light network; membership bundle; brand + capital | **W:** evidence gap; false-positive cascade costs; no owned clinical follow-up layer; integration risk |
| External | **O:** 1,000-site rollout; employer/insurer channels; international replication (UK proven); MI Lab data moat | **T:** ACR/radiology backlash and possible FTC-style advertising scrutiny; Prenuvo/Neko price-point competition; payer non-coverage keeps TAM discretionary; a high-profile missed-cancer or overdiagnosis story[^31][^32][^33] |

## 10a. Business-model lessons (unit-economics view)

- **Scanner-time is the whole game.** At a ballpark US$400–600/hr fully loaded cost of partner MRI time *(analyst estimate from outpatient benchmarks)*, a 60-minute scan cannot profitably retail much below ~US$1,000 — which is why 2019-era Ezra sat at US$1,950. Flash's 22-minute protocol puts ~2.7 scans in the same hour, making US$499 retail workable with radiologist-read costs amortised by volume[^18][^19] *(arithmetic is analyst inference; components cited)*.
- **Membership converts a purchase into a base rate.** Function's US$365/yr fee creates ~annual touchpoints and a pre-qualified audience for the US$499 attach; scan CAC approaches zero for existing members. This inverted the historical problem (Ezra spending to sell one-off US$2,000 scans)[^12][^16].
- **Price cuts were announced as clinical news.** Every FDA clearance doubled as a pricing press release — regulatory milestones were converted directly into marketing moments[^18][^19].
- **Vertical vs horizontal trade.** Prenuvo (owned clinics) protects experience and margin but scales in single-digit site counts per year; Ezra (partners) scaled to ~100 sites with 1,000 planned but doesn't control patient experience at the magnet. Neko splits the difference with owned clinics at a radically cheaper non-MRI sensor stack[^14][^28][^41].
- **The follow-up gap is a liability and an opportunity.** None of the three owns downstream care. Reviewers flag exactly this (anxiety after findings, out-of-network workups)[^17][^30]. An operator that owns follow-up medicine captures LTV the scanners leave behind.

## 11. Will they enter Asia?

No announced plans for Ezra/Function or Neko in Asia as of July 2026; Ezra's international proof-of-concept is the UK (Alliance Medical partnership, 11 sites)[^20][^21]. Three observations sharpen the Asia read:

- The UK entry shows Function's preferred internationalisation mechanics: one national imaging partner, capital-light, brand-forward — replicable in Singapore or Malaysia within months of a decision, not years *(analysis)*[^20].
- Asia-specific pull already exists: Singapore consumers pay SGD 4,388–5,014 for locally-read full-body MRI with no AI acceleration and no membership wrapper, i.e. the pre-Flash 2019 US market state (pricing evidence in the [Singapore dossier](singapore-longevity-clinics.md)).
- Regulatory friction is the brake: elective whole-body screening sits inside licensing and advertising regimes (MOH Singapore, MOH Malaysia) that have no US-style DTC imaging precedent, favouring locally licensed operators as partners of record *(inference)*. The realistic near-term Asian event is **Prenuvo Singapore** (entity incorporated August 2025, opening timing unannounced)[^38][^39]. Function's asset-light model could enter SEA fast via radiology partnerships if it chose — Singapore private imaging capacity already exists at SGD 4,388–5,014 retail (see [Singapore dossier](singapore-longevity-clinics.md)) — making pre-emptive local partnership lock-up a live strategic question for Welltech *(analysis)*.

## 12. Implications for Welltech — copy / avoid / localise

**Copy:**
1. **Membership-first, scan-second architecture.** Function's US$365/yr biomarker membership is the retention and data spine; imaging is an attach. Welltech's SEA translation: an SGD/MYR-priced annual diagnostics membership with GLP-1/metabolic care as the clinical anchor, imaging as a partner-delivered add-on.
2. **Asset-light imaging via partner radiology.** Ezra never bought a scanner; Welltech shouldn't either. Contract off-peak capacity with SG/MY private imaging groups at member rates (the RadNet play)[^14][^15].
3. **Price-transparency as marketing.** Every player in this set publishes prices; almost no SEA incumbent does ([Malaysia dossier](malaysia-longevity-clinics.md)). Published, instalment-friendly pricing is a proven conversion weapon[^16].
4. **Waitlist/scarcity and equity-for-influence CAC.** Neko's waitlist and Function's celebrity cap table are cheap-CAC blueprints adaptable to SEA KOLs and corporate-wellness channels[^10][^41].

**Avoid:**
1. **Leading with whole-body MRI claims.** The evidence backlash (ACR, Fred Hutch, RSNA) will reach Asia; MOH-regulated markets punish over-claiming. Frame imaging as physician-gated risk stratification, not "scan everything"[^31][^33].
2. **Single-ticket scan economics.** Ezra at US$1,950 grew slowly for six years; growth came only when the scan became a US$499 attach to a membership — don't build the business on SGD 4,000 one-offs[^5][^18].
3. **Owning the false-positive cascade unprepared.** ~5% suspicious-finding rates require a follow-up care pathway (specialist network, counselling, costs) designed before launch, or reviews will document abandonment[^17][^30].

5. **Regulatory milestones as marketing.** Ezra turned each FDA clearance into a price-cut announcement; Welltech's SEA equivalents (HSA/MOH registrations, clinical-outcome publications like NOVI's IJO paper) should be productised the same way[^18][^19].
6. **Doctor-in-the-loop as a differentiator.** Neko includes a physician debrief in every £299 scan and has the longest waitlist of the set — evidence that human clinical interpretation, not just imaging, is what mass-affluent consumers queue for[^40][^41].

**Avoid (continued):**

4. **US-grade CAC assumptions.** Celebrity equity and 100k-waitlist theatrics are US/EU phenomena; SEA CAC will run through corporate wellness, insurer partnerships, physician referral and WhatsApp-shareable reports instead *(analysis)*.

**Localise:** SEA's advantage is that Welltech can own what the US models lack — the longitudinal treatment layer (telehealth, GLP-1, TRT, WhatsApp-first coordination) that turns a finding into a managed patient. Function bolted imaging onto labs; Welltech can bolt both onto care. Target price architecture *(analyst estimate)*: membership at SGD 500–800/yr (Function-equivalent purchasing-power position), partner MRI attach at SGD 1,500–2,500 (undercutting today's SGD 4,388–5,014 retail), before Prenuvo lands and anchors the top of the market.

**Bottom line.** Ezra is the proof that AI plus asset-light distribution collapses preventive-imaging prices by ~75%, and Function is the proof that a sub-US$500 annual diagnostics membership is the right container for it. Neither owns the medicine that follows a finding. Welltech should treat the scan as a commodity someone else's magnet performs, the membership as the business, and follow-up care — the piece all three global models externalise — as its defensible layer in Southeast Asia.
